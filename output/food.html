Airman’s Attic on Peterson offers free home goods, uniforms

📅 Nov 01, 2026  |  Category: Food  |  Read Source →

A little retail therapy on a stressful day can go a long way, particularly if you feel like you walked away with a good deal.

The Airman’s Attic on Peterson Space Force Base gives junior military members and their families that hit of dopamine for free.

At the little shop, service members with ranks of E-5 and below and their families can pick up five items each week at no cost. The shop is open to all junior service members in the region, including sergeants and below in the Army and Space Force and staff sergeants and below in the Air Force.

Some of the goods fall into the critical category, like baby wipes, while others brighten up your home or your day, such as a lamp or a new picture book for your child. 

When young troops come through the door, they may also get encouraging words from Rackel Haskell and Cherlyn Griggs, the longtime volunteer co-managers. Both military spouses, the two have spent decades running the shop and know it can be tough for young airmen fresh out of basic training living away from home for the first time. 

“I actually give them hugs,” said Haskell, who drives in from Elbert to run the shop. 

Rackel Haskell is one of two co-managers at the Airman’s Attic on Peterson Space Force Base. (Mary Shinn, The Gazette)

Her co-manager, Griggs, is more likely to offer a bit of cooking advice if young troops are walking out with an item from the grab-and-go food section, in addition to a little nudge toward maturity. 

“I am the one that says, ‘Put your big boy pants on and buck up,’” she said with a laugh. 

The two started Peterson’s first food pantry in the Attic with their own money. It’s an endeavor that the Peterson, Schriever, Cheyenne Mountain Spouses’ Club now runs in a larger space. The Attic maintains a small but popular grab-and-go section.

Other items that line the shelves, such as cups, bowls, Christmas decorations, toys, children’s clothes and other items, come from donations. When service members move, particularly during the summer, donations flow in at such high numbers that the boxes can be lined up outside the building. 

“During the summer, it’s crazy,” Griggs said.  

Service members also pass along their gently used uniform items, such as fatigues, dress blues and boots. The items fill a whole room and can help offset the high cost of buying a whole uniform. A new dress jacket on the Army and Air Force Exchange Services website currently sells for $192. 

A room at Airman’s Attic is dedicated to uniform items that service members can pick up for free. (Mary Shinn, The Gazette)

The shop’s rank limit does not apply to the uniform items and its popular aspect of the Attic, Haskell explained. 

On a single day in October, the uniform section saw the most junior troops up through the equivalent of an Air Force colonel come through, she said. 

Service members can pick up as many items as they need from this room, and it will only count as one item on their limit of five, she explained. 

Running the shop relies on the Attic’s team of 17 volunteers who sort and clean the donations, among many other tasks.

If they receive items they cannot put on their own shelves, they will give them to those who can use them. For example, blankets that come in dirty go on to provide comfort to animals at the Humane Society of the Pikes Peak region, Griggs said. 

After decades of managing the shop, burnout hasn’t caught up to the cheerful managers.

All the volunteers enjoy each other’s company and will spend time together at potlucks and other events, they explained. 

“We love it,” Griggs said.

The Airmen’s Attic on Peterson is open Monday through Wednesday, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. at 1525 Selfridge St.
