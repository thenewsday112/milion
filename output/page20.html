StagePass Artist Spotlight Bill McClintock – The Art of the Mashup Taking Songs That Should Never Work Together... And Making Them Work

There are mashups... and then there are Bill McClintock mashups.

Bill McClintock has built a devoted following on YouTube by doing something that sounds simple until you actually hear him do it.... taking two songs from completely different musical worlds and somehow making them sound like they belong together.

RATT and Marvin Gaye. Huey Lewis and Metallica. Hall & Oates and Nine Inch Nails. Slayer and Katrina & The Waves. These are combinations that should have absolutely no business working together.... yet somehow, they do.

And that "somehow" is where Bill's musical ability comes in.

Bill is an experienced guitarist with more than 20 years of playing behind him and holds a degree in music from Duquesne University, with concentrations in Music Technology and Music Education. He is also an elementary general music teacher. While he never had a strong desire to perform in front of an audience, he found another way to share his creativity.... through the art of the mashup.

What began as a way to combine his love of music, technology and creativity eventually developed into a YouTube phenomenon. His videos have attracted hundreds of thousands, and in some cases millions, of views. InsideHook described McClintock as a YouTube sensation whose mashups were receiving between 200,000 and four million views at the time of its 2021 feature.

There Is More To It Than Putting Two Songs Together

The magic behind Bill's work is not simply choosing two funny or unexpected songs. There is a surprising amount of musical craftsmanship involved.

Bill has explained that he generally looks for songs that are in the same key and have roughly the same tempo. From there, the real work begins. He can try hundreds of combinations before finding two songs that genuinely sound as though they belong together.

He also pays close attention to rhythm, vocal phrasing, harmonic context and the way individual musical sections interact. Guitar solos, additional musical elements and unexpected changes can be added to keep a mashup from becoming predictable.

The result is something more sophisticated than a simple A + B formula. Bill's mashups often create the strange sensation that you are hearing a familiar song for the first time.

"I try hundreds of combinations until I find two songs that sound like they belong together."

The Mashup That Put Bill On The Map

One of Bill's most recognizable creations is "I Heard it Round and Round the Grapevine," which combines Marvin Gaye's classic "I Heard It Through the Grapevine" with RATT's "Round and Round."

On paper, the combination sounds ridiculous. Motown soul and '80s hair metal are about as far apart as two musical styles can get. Yet when Marvin Gaye's vocal is placed over RATT's hard-rock instrumental, something unexpected happens.... it works.

The mashup attracted attention from music publications and was shared and discussed by fans and music outlets. Even more remarkably, RATT drummer Bobby Blotzer reached out to Bill to tell him how much he enjoyed the mashup.

It became a perfect example of what makes McClintock's work so much fun. The joke gets your attention.... but the musicality is what keeps you listening.

Musician

Bill has been playing guitar for more than 20 years and holds a degree in music from Duquesne University.

Mashup Artist

His work combines vocals, instruments and musical styles that seem completely incompatible... until Bill puts them together.

YouTube Sensation

His mashups have reached audiences numbering in the hundreds of thousands and, for some videos, into the millions.

Unexpected Is The Point

Perhaps the best way to understand Bill McClintock is to understand that the unexpected is not a side effect of his work.... it is the entire point.

He isn't looking for two songs that obviously belong together. He wants the opposite. The stranger the combination, the greater the challenge. And when the musical pieces suddenly lock into place, the result can be both hilarious and genuinely impressive.

That's why Bill's mashups have such a broad appeal. You might click because you want to hear what happens when Marvin Gaye meets RATT.... but you stay because you're hearing something that is musically clever, technically precise and completely unexpected.

Bill McClintock has taken the mashup from a simple internet novelty and turned it into a creative musical art form all his own.

Now Hear What All The Fuss Is About...

Before we get to our interview with Bill, take a look at some of his incredible mashups. Once you hear them, you'll understand why we had to make Bill McClintock a StagePass Artist Spotlight.

Want to see more videos? Please support Bill's work by subscribing to his YouTube Channel https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC6C3PX8hLzueaPWW9nmT85w

Facebook Page
https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100027589669064

Our Interview with Bill McClintock

Ok, first of all, we’d just like to say we are big fans of your work. How long have you been doing Mashups? What started you on this path?

Bill: Thank you! That’s great to hear! I’ve been doing mash-ups for about one year now. Music has always been my thing (I’ve been playing guitar for 20+ years). However, I’ve never had the desire to perform in front of an audience. I saw making mash-ups as an opportunity to share my creativity and musical ability with an audience without actually having to be in front of people. This was after being inspired by some incredible mash-ups like Danzig’s “Mother” with Steely Dan’s “Dirty Work,” The Beatles’ “Imagine” with Van Halen’s “Jump,” and Ozzy’s “Crazy Train” with Earth, Wind & Fire’s “September.”

You seem to have a good grasp of key and tempo. Are you a musician? Do you have any musical training?

Bill: Yes. I’ve been playing guitar for the past 20 years or so and I have a degree in music from Duquesne University with a concentration in both Music Technology and Music Education.

As far as copyright laws are concerned you seem to work in a gray area. I wasn’t able to find anything specific that would preclude you from doing Mashups. Have you run into any issues with copyright? Has any artist or lawyer told you to cease and desist? Can they? I mean do they have legal footing to do so in your opinion?

Bill: YouTube has this magical thing called Content ID that somehow detects when a user uploads copyrighted material, including audio and video. Content ID has detected copyrighted material in just about every mash-up I have published to YouTube. When this happens, YouTube sends me an email as a heads-up to let me know that I used copyrighted material in my video. They don’t block the video, though, because I have agreed to run ads on my videos. Any revenue generated by my videos goes directly to the artists who own the rights to the music I use.

I have not been contacted by any artists or lawyers for any legal reasons—not yet, anyway! One artist­­—Bobby Blotzer of RATT—did contact me to tell me how much he enjoyed my mash-up of “Round and Round” with Marvin Gaye’s “I Heard it Through the Grapevine.” He was also hoping I could mash more RATT songs with Motown songs with the intention of recording a RATT mash-up album.

I’ve noticed that the tunes that you mash are so far apart when it comes to style etc. and yet you make them work perfectly. How do you come up with the ideas? Do songs have to be in the same key? What is similar enough that you think these two songs will work well together?

Bill: My goal is always to mash two or more songs that should in no way, shape or form go together, and make them work. I’ve found that this is the key to blowing peoples’ minds!

Musical style really isn’t important when finding two songs that are compatible. First and foremost, the two songs need to be in the same key and have the same tempo, or at least be close. I use music software that can adjust the key and tempo of a song to make it match exactly with another song.

Another important aspect is rhythm. By that I mean that there should be rhythms in both the vocal line and the instrumental track that work well together. This is something that is difficult to explain but it can definitely be heard and contributes to a “good fit” between two songs.

The timing of the vocal line with all of the musical elements in the instrumental track is crucial. If a vocal phrase is two measures long, the instrumental track should allow for those two measures without any fills or accents that would detract from the vocals. This is difficult to explain without an example. Listen to the mash-up I did of the Guess Who’s “American Woman” with Earth, Wind & Fire’s “Shining Star.” There is a four measure section that has a horn accent on the “and of 4” in each measure. There is just enough space at the end of each vocal phrase that allows for those accents- “Don’t come a-hangin’ around my door” <accent> “I don’t wanna see your face no more” <accent> etc.

One more thing that I absolutely love is taking a vocal line that was originally sung over just one chord and putting it with an instrumental track that has chord changes. Hearing a vocal line in a different harmonic context makes the vocal line itself sound different, even though I didn’t change it at all! Some good examples of this concept can be found in the Eric Clapton/Michael Jackson and Gwen Stefani/Bee Gee’s mash-ups I did.

Do you use specialized software? Are you ok with giving them a plug or would you prefer to keep it a trade secret?

Bill: I do. I use The Amazing Slowdowner to adjust tempo and key, Melodyne to adjust individual pitches, and Garageband to mix everything together. All of these things could easily be accomplished with one program like Logic Pro X, but I still haven’t upgraded. Once I have the audio finished, I use iMovie to edit the videos from each song together with the music.

Which of your mashups got the most views?

Bill: “I Heard it Round and Round the Grapevine.” This actually really surprised me because I personally didn’t think it was over the top awesome like so many people did. I can see why people like it, though. First of all, it’s Marvin Gaye with RATT, which is hysterical, and just wrong on so many levels. Marvin’s high notes are somehow transformed to heavy metal-like screams when they are put into a metal context. And, by some miracle, the rhythms of Marvin’s vocal line fit better with the “Round and Round” instrumental track than Stephen Pearcy’s. I honestly just got lucky there. Didn’t have to change a thing.

What are you working on right now?

Bill: Nothing. Just kidding. I’m currently gathering ideas to try to find a good match. I would like to do another classic rock, yacht rock, or Motown vocal with a metal instrumental track. That’s one of my favorite formulas. But, you’ll have to wait and see!

Thank you Bill for taking the time to do this.

vor 9 Jahren IT-Sicherheit und Security Awareness in KMU

Der Bundesverband IT-Mittelstand e.V. (BITMi) und die Fachgruppe IT-Sicherheit des BITMi laden am 9. April 2018 um 13:00 Uhr zur Vorstellung der Studienergebnisse „Aktuelle Lage der IT-Sicherheit in KMU“. Die Veranstaltung findet im Innovation Center des GRÜN Software HUB statt.

Im Zeitalter der Digitalisierung und Vernetzung nimmt auch das Thema IT-Sicherheit einen immer größeren Stellenwert ein. Kleine und mittlere Unternehmen (KMU) machen den größten Anteil der deutschen Wirtschaftaus und werden in der vernetzen Welt von morgen bedeutsam bleiben. So innovativ sie oft sind, verfügen sie doch im Gegensatz zu großen Unternehmen meist über nur eingeschränkte Ressourcen für IT-Sicherheit.

Das Wissenschaftliche Institut für Infrastruktur und Kommunikationsdienste (WIK) hat im Rahmen der Initiative „IT-Sicherheit in der Wirtschaft“ des Bundesministeriums für Wirtschaft und Energie (BMWi) die Studie „Aktuelle Lage der IT-Sicherheit in KMU“ veröffentlicht. Mit Unterstützung des BITMi möchte das WIK seine Projektergebnisse bekannt machen, die KMU sensibilisieren und Handlungsoptionen für die Unternehmen aufzeigen.

Die Studie auf Basis einer Repräsentativbefragung bei 1.508 KMU in Deutschland zeigt: Trotz zunehmender Digitalisierung mangelt es immer noch am Bewusstsein für IT-Sicherheit. Selbst da, wo das eigene Risiko als hoch gilt, wird nur unzureichend für Schutz gesorgt. Darüber hinaus liefert die Studie Erkenntnisse über das Sicherheitsniveau und leitet Empfehlungen ab, wie die IT-Sicherheit in KMU erhöht werden kann.

Im Anschluss an die Präsentation und Diskussion der Studie bieten wir Ihnen zudem die Möglichkeit, an einem Security-Awareness-Circle-Training „Out of the Box“ teilzunehmen und dadurch weitere Erkenntnisse über die Lage der IT-Sicherheit in Ihrem Unternehmen zu gewinnen.

Beim Security-Awareness-Parcours durchlaufen Teams mit 3 bis 12 Teilnehmern synchron die vorhandenen Themenstationen, an denen sie von Moderatoren hinsichtlich verschiedener Sicherheitsthemen sensibilisiert werden. Wir laden Sie dazu ein, bei diesem interaktiven Format die Stationen Cyber Security und Social Engineering zu durchlaufen und Ihre Awareness für IT-Sicherheit und den konkreten Handlungsbedarf in Ihrem Unternehmen zu verbessern.

Die Veranstaltung richtet sich an alle BITMi-Mitglieder, Geschäftsführer und Entscheider sowie Interessenten und ist kostenfrei.

Alexandr Misko – New-Age Experimental Percussive Fingerstyle Guitar Redefining how an Acoustic Guitar Can be played Alexandr Misko – New-Age Experimental Percussive Fingerstyle Guitarist

Meet Alexandr Misko

Alexandr Misko is a self-described "New-age experimental percussive fingerstyle Guitar player" from Russia who has developed a remarkably distinctive approach to the acoustic guitar.

His musical journey began at the age of 12 when his parents gave him his first classical guitar on New Year's Eve. Before that, Alexandr admits that much of his time was spent playing video games on his PlayStation. But the arrival of that guitar changed everything.

Alexandr began taking classical guitar lessons and spent three years studying the instrument. However, after discovering the world of fingerstyle guitar, he eventually left his formal lessons behind and began developing his own sound through independent exploration and experimentation.

By the age of 20, Alexandr had already become one of the most exciting young percussive fingerstyle guitarists in the world. His playing combines fingerstyle, tapping, percussion, rhythm, unusual time signatures, polyrhythms and innovative techniques that push the acoustic guitar far beyond traditional expectations.

With millions of views on YouTube and a growing international audience, Alexandr's music demonstrates that technical ability and emotional songwriting do not have to exist separately. His goal is not simply to show what he can do with a guitar. Instead, he wants to create music that makes people feel something.

An Interview With Alexandr Misko

Alexandr's music is difficult to place into a single category. His influences stretch from American hip-hop and old-school rap to deathcore, progressive metal and doom metal. At the same time, he draws inspiration from minimalist and avant-garde composers including Steve Reich, Erik Satie, Philip Glass, Graham Fitkin and John Cage.

His guitar influences include some of the most respected names in modern fingerstyle guitar, including Don Ross, Mike Dawes, Jon Gomm, Pino Forastiere, Thomas Leeb and Preston Reed.

However, Alexandr is careful not to simply imitate the artists who inspire him. He believes that drawing inspiration from many different genres allows him to create something that is uniquely his own.

Rather than defining himself solely by his technical abilities, Alexandr describes the genre of his music as "Progressive Pop." His goal is to create music that remains melodic and accessible while incorporating progressive elements such as complicated structures, unusual time signatures and polyrhythms.

STAGE PASS was recently able to pry Alexandr away from writing his university exams to tell us more about himself.

The Interview

So tell us, what age did you first start playing guitar?

Alexandr: I started playing guitar at the age of 12. My parents presented me my first classical guitar on the New Year’s Eve. Before that I was doing nothing and just playing videogames all day long. So now I’m 20 and I’m so glad that I gave up playing playstation back then ha-ha!

What first interested you in playing the guitar?

Alexandr: I have never asked anybody to buy me a guitar or any other musical instrument, but it was obvious that I have always shown interest in music since early childhood. I was really into American hip-hop and rap music of East & West coast! Moreover, I tried to find biographies and lyrics, and sometimes I used to record some of my raps on my phone along with my friend. We were mimicking American slang that we had been hearing on those songs and tried to come up with something similar to be cool. That was fun! I guess that at the age of 10-12 was the first spark.

Were you self taught or did you take lessons?

Alexandr: When I got my first guitar I decided to take private lessons with a tutor straight away. We were studying classical guitar for 3 years and to be honest I have never been an excellent student. But when I finally got acquainted with “fingerstyle”, I completely abandoned my lessons and started to learn it on my own.

Your guitar playing covers a variety of styles including fingerstyle and tapping yet also incorporates rhythm. I don't think I've seen people use the tuning peg to make note adjustments while playing a song before. What were your influences and how did you end up with the style that seems to be uniquely yours?

Alexandr: Basically everything that I can do now is a blend of other guitarists’ styles combined. I know one thing for sure - I couldn't do anything decent without learning songs and gaining experience from such great composers as Don Ross, Mike Dawes, Jon Gomm, Pino Forastiere, Thomas Leeb, Preston Reed, Steve Reich and many others. They are all my heroes and have been inspiring me since then.

But of course I get inspiration not only from other guitarists because it can make you sound exactly like your idol, and for sure you don’t want to be someone’s musical clone. That’s why I’m always trying to find something new and interesting in other genres that can be really far away from guitar playing and to use it to make my own tunes unique.

For example, I’m fond of heavy genres like Deathcore, Prog metal, Doom Metal. Also I love minimalistic avant-garde music of 19th and 20th centuries, for example such composers as Steve Reich, Erik Satie, Philip Glass, Graham Fitkin, John Cage. I still love American old school rap and write some lyrics in Russian…

Eventually I will be able to name at least one favorite artist in every existing genre of music. At least that's my goal

What do you personally call your style of guitar playing?

Alexandr: There are so many things in my style of playing so an appropriate name would be long and boring like “New-age experimental percussive fingerstyle”, but it roughly describes only technical side of my music.

I try to write music, not a technique, so in terms of genre I would call it “Progressive Pop” because I’m trying to write melodic and accessible music with some progressive flavor that means the use of complicated structures, unusual time signatures, polyrhythms and other stuff.

Thank you Alexandr for taking the time to do this.

"I try to write music, not a technique."

About Alexandr Misko

Alexandr Misko's story is a fascinating example of how a musician can take inspiration from seemingly unrelated places and transform it into something completely original.

His foundation in classical guitar gave him the technical discipline to understand the instrument, while his discovery of fingerstyle opened the door to a much broader creative world. From there, he began incorporating influences from hip-hop, rap, metal, minimalist composition and experimental music.

The result is a style that turns the acoustic guitar into something far more than a traditional melodic instrument. Alexandr uses the guitar's body as percussion, incorporates tapping and rhythm into his playing, and even manipulates tuning during performances to create sounds that are both surprising and musical.

Perhaps most importantly, Alexandr's philosophy remains focused on songwriting. For him, the technique is simply a tool. The ultimate goal is to create music that is melodic, accessible and capable of provoking emotion and vivid imagery in the listener.

The Story Behind "Wake Up Call"

Some songs entertain us. Others tell a story. Wake Up Call does something far more profound—it gives voice to unimaginable loss while offering a message of hope.

Written by acclaimed singer, songwriter and producer Jamie Sparks, the song was inspired by the tragic loss of his son, Josiah Sparks. Rather than allowing grief to become the final chapter, Jamie transformed heartbreak into music that encourages compassion, healing and a renewed appreciation for life.

"I wrote this song for a few reasons. Wake Up Call was written in response to the violence that claimed the life of my son Josiah Sparks."

Jamie explains that the tragedy forced him to confront realities he had once known but no longer witnessed every day. Growing up surrounded by violence, poverty and hardship, he eventually moved away for work and built a life where those circumstances were no longer part of his everyday experience. After Josiah's death, however, those same social issues became impossible to ignore.

The second reason for releasing the song was deeply personal. Jamie wanted to honour his son's memory while reminding listeners that violence never affects just one victim. Every life taken leaves behind grieving parents, siblings, relatives, friends and entire communities forever changed.

Perhaps the most touching inspiration came from Josiah himself. Before his passing, he often encouraged his father to return to making music. Jamie had not released new material in several years, and through this project he fulfilled a calling his son had reminded him not to abandon.

Today, Wake Up Call serves not only as a memorial to Josiah but also as an invitation for all of us to love more deeply, forgive more readily, reconnect with those we've drifted away from, and remember that tomorrow is never guaranteed.

vor 9 Jahren Weihnachtsfeier GRÜN Christmas Rock

Eine Weihnachtsfeier im Kreise der Kollegen gehört zur festen Tradition der GRÜN Software AG. Diese Tradition will das Unternehmen natürlich auch in diesem Jahr fortsetzen. Die diesjährige Weihnachtsfeier findet am 15. Dezember 2017 statt.

Startschuss der Weihnachtsfeier sind die alljährlichen Winterperspektiven, die im Innovation Center des GRÜN Software HUBs stattfinden. Im Anschluss an die Weihnachtsfeier wird jedoch jeder Mitarbeiter der GRÜN Software AG ein wenig zum Gastgeber. Denn dann steigt ab 22.30 Uhr unser GRÜN Christmas Rock mit allen Mitarbeitern, Freunden von Mitarbeitern, Kunden, Lieferanten und Interessenten. Jeder bei GRÜN lädt 10 Freunde ein, wenn er mag. Und die GRÜN Software AG selbst lädt natürlich auch Kunden, Interessenten, Lieferanten und Freunde ein. Der Eintritt zu dieser geschlossenen Gesellschaft ist natürlich frei, die Getränke werden nach einer Preisliste ausgegeben.

GRÜN Christmas Rock findet in der DIGITAL CHURCH, der ehemaligen St. Elisabeth Kirche in der Jülicher Straße 72a in Aachen, statt. Die DIGITAL CHURCH ist das Herzstück des digitalHUB Aachen e.V., der im Juli diesen Jahres die Digitalisierungsinitiative „Aachen digitalisiert!“ gestartet und das erste CoWorking Deutschlands in einem Kirchenschiff eröffnet hat. Die GRÜN Software AG war an der Gründung des digitalHUB Aachen als Mit-Initiator beteiligt.

Die Weihnachtsparty GRÜN Christmas Rock findet unregelmäßig im Abstand von mehreren Jahren statt, zuletzt 2011.

Anmeldungen zu einer möglichen Teilnahme können über das Online-Formular oder über Facebook abgegeben werden. Der Einlass erfolgt nur nach Bestätigung der Anmeldung über Teilnehmerlisten oder durch Vorlage einer Original-Einladungskarte. Letztere versenden wir postalisch an einen ausgewählten Verteiler in den kommenden Tagen.
