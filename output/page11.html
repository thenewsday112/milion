Die Planung und Durchführung von Veranstaltungen hat sich in den letzten Jahren grundlegend gewandelt. Wo früher dicke Aktenordner, unzählige Excel-Tabellen und handschriftliche Notizen den Alltag in Event-Locations prägten, setzen heute immer mehr Betreiber auf digitale Lösungen. Die Erwartungshaltung der Kunden ist gestiegen: Schnelligkeit, Professionalität und eine reibungslose Kommunikation werden vorausgesetzt. Wer hier noch manuell arbeitet, verliert schnell den Anschluss an den Wettbewerb und riskiert Fehler in der Buchungslogistik.

Die Digitalisierung bietet jedoch weit mehr als nur die bloße Ablösung von Papier. Es geht darum, komplexe Prozesse zu vereinfachen und Transparenz für das gesamte Team zu schaffen. Wenn alle Beteiligten – vom Vertrieb über das Catering bis hin zur Technik – in Echtzeit auf denselben Informationsstand zugreifen können, sinkt die Fehlerquote gegen Null. Dies ist die Grundlage für ein erstklassiges Gästeerlebnis und eine effiziente Auslastung der Räumlichkeiten.

Effizienzsteigerung durch spezialisierte IT-Lösungen

Für Betreiber von Veranstaltungszentren, Hotels mit Tagungsräumen oder besonderen Off-Locations ist die Wahl der richtigen IT-Infrastruktur entscheidend. Eine professionelle Management Software für Event Locations ermöglicht es, Anfragen zentral zu verwalten, Angebote innerhalb von Minuten zu erstellen und die Verfügbarkeit von Ressourcen sofort zu prüfen. Statt Zeit mit administrativen Routineaufgaben zu verschwenden, können sich die Mitarbeiter wieder auf das Wesentliche konzentrieren: die Beratung des Kunden und die kreative Ausgestaltung der Events.

Ein weiterer entscheidender Vorteil ist die Automatisierung von Schnittstellen. Moderne Systeme lassen sich nahtlos in bestehende Webseiten integrieren, sodass Kunden direkt online Anfragen stellen können, die sofort im Backoffice landen. Das spart nicht nur Zeit, sondern erhöht auch die Conversion-Rate, da die Reaktionszeit auf Kundenanfragen drastisch verkürzt wird. In einer Branche, in der der Erste oft den Zuschlag erhält, ist dies ein unschätzbarer Marktvorteil.

Die Rolle von Daten und Analysen im Eventmanagement

Neben der reinen Organisation spielt die Datenauswertung eine immer wichtigere Rolle. Durch die zentrale Erfassung aller Buchungsvorgänge entstehen wertvolle Datenpools. Betreiber können genau analysieren, welche Zeiträume besonders gefragt sind, welche Pakete die höchsten Margen erzielen und wo eventuelle Engpässe in der Ressourcenplanung liegen. Diese Erkenntnisse sind die Basis für fundierte unternehmerische Entscheidungen und eine langfristige Wachstumsstrategie.

Um diese Potenziale voll auszuschöpfen, bedarf es einer intuitiven Benutzeroberfläche, die von allen Teammitgliedern ohne langwierige Schulungen bedient werden kann. Die Tools von MICE Operations zeigen hierbei eindrucksvoll, wie Komplexität reduziert werden kann, ohne an Funktionstiefe einzubüßen. Die Cloud-basierte Architektur sorgt zudem dafür, dass der Zugriff ortsunabhängig möglich ist – ein Muss in der heutigen mobilen Arbeitswelt.

Nachhaltigkeit und Zukunftsfähigkeit in der Branche

Ein oft unterschätzter Aspekt der Digitalisierung ist der Beitrag zur Nachhaltigkeit. Durch den Verzicht auf papierbasierte Prozesse und die Optimierung der Logistik können Ressourcen geschont werden. Digitale Raumpläne und Catering-Listen reduzieren den Verschnitt und sorgen für eine präzisere Planung. Dies spart nicht nur Kosten, sondern zahlt auch auf das grüne Image einer Location ein, was für viele Firmenkunden mittlerweile ein hartes Auswahlkriterium bei der Buchung darstellt.

Die technologische Entwicklung steht nicht still. Künstliche Intelligenz wird in Zukunft dabei helfen, Buchungstrends noch genauer vorherzusagen oder die Kommunikation mit Kunden über intelligente Chatbots weiter zu automatisieren. Wer heute die Basis mit einer soliden digitalen Struktur legt, ist für diese kommenden Innovationen bestens gerüstet. Es geht darum, eine flexible Umgebung zu schaffen, die mit den Anforderungen der Kunden und den Möglichkeiten der Technik mitwächst. Zo können Veranstaltungsorte ihre Resilienz stärken und auch in dynamischen Marktphasen erfolgreich agieren.

Damit kann das Pilotprojekt zur regulierten Aufbereitung ausgewählter Einmal-Medizinprodukte konkret anlaufen. Zugelassen wurden zehn öffentliche und private Gesundheitseinrichtungen mit insgesamt 15 EP-Kliniken. Ziel der Pilotphase ist es, die technische Machbarkeit der regulierten Aufbereitung bestimmter Einmal-Medizinprodukte im Klinikalltag zu bewerten und zu prüfen, ob das Modell perspektivisch dauerhaft etabliert und erweitert werden kann. Das Pilotprojekt läuft bis zum 1. September 2028 und wird durch einen Lenkungsausschuss sowie ein Bewertungskomitee unter Aufsicht der französischen Generaldirektion für Gesundheit begleitet.

Klare Vorgaben für die Umsetzung

Mit den nun veröffentlichten Vorgaben wird klarer, wie das Pilotprojekt im Klinikalltag umgesetzt werden soll. Geregelt werden unter anderem die Zusammenarbeit der teilnehmenden Gesundheitseinrichtungen mit Vanguard als einzigem qualifizierten externen Aufbereitungsunternehmen, die vertraglichen Grundlagen sowie die Patienteninformation.

Für die praktische Umsetzung kann Vanguard bereits auf bestehende Erfahrungen in Frankreich aufbauen: Mit rund der Hälfte der teilnehmenden EP-Kliniken arbeitet Vanguard schon heute bei der Einsammlung gebrauchter Medizinprodukte zusammen. Nun gilt es, weitere Kliniken in den Prozess einzubinden, die vertraglichen Grundlagen für den Einsatz aufbereiteter Medizinprodukte abzuschließen und die Abläufe so vorzubereiten, dass erste Verkäufe und Anwendungen bei Patienten ab September 2026 umgesetzt werden können.

Bedeutung für die Elektrophysiologie

Für die Elektrophysiologie ist das Pilotprojekt besonders relevant. Gerade in diesem Bereich kommen komplexe Einmal-Medizinprodukte zum Einsatz, bei denen Kreislaufmodelle einen wichtigen Beitrag zu Ressourcenschonung, Abfallvermeidung und Versorgungssicherheit leisten können.

Medical Remanufacturing eröffnet die Möglichkeit, geeignete Produkte in einem regulierten und qualitätsgesicherten Prozess erneut nutzbar zu machen. Frankreich prüft nun in der Praxis, wie sich diese Form der Aufbereitung in bestehende Klinikabläufe integrieren lässt und welche ökologischen, organisatorischen und wirtschaftlichen Effekte damit verbunden sind.

Ein wichtiger Schritt für Medical Remanufacturing in Europa

Für Vanguard ist der Start des Pilotprojekts ein wichtiges Signal – für Frankreich und für die europäische Entwicklung von Medical Remanufacturing insgesamt. Was in zehn westlichen europäischen Ländern bereits seit Jahren möglich und in der Praxis etabliert ist, erhält mit dem französischen Pilotprojekt weiteren Rückenwind.

Das französische Pilotprojekt zeigt auch, wie wichtig ein einheitlicher europäischer Rahmen ist. Die Pilotphase betrachtet sowohl die CE-Aufbereitung als auch die Aufbereitung nach den Common Specifications und macht damit deutlich, welches Potenzial regulierte Aufbereitung für Europa hat. Wenn die jeweiligen Anforderungen erfüllt, Prozesse qualitätsgesichert umgesetzt und Produkte nachvollziehbar freigegeben werden, sollte Medical Remanufacturing künftig einfacher und verlässlicher in Europa eingesetzt werden können. Die Weiterentwicklung der EU-Medizinprodukteverordnung bietet dafür eine wichtige Chance.

Das IJsselmeer gehört zu den beliebtesten Revieren für einen Segelurlaub in den Niederlanden. Du bekommst viel Wasserfläche, gut ausgebaute Häfen und meist kurze Distanzen. Das ist perfekt, wenn du Segeln auf dem IJsselmeer für Anfänger planst, aber auch spannend für erfahrene Crews, die längere Schläge mögen. Ein weiterer Vorteil: Wenn das Wetter umschlägt, erreichst du oft schnell einen sicheren Hafen. So bleibt der Törn planbar, ohne dass er langweilig wird.

Warum das IJsselmeer auch für Anfänger passt

Das IJsselmeer war früher ein Meeresarm und wurde durch den Abschlussdeich vom Wattenmeer getrennt. Heute ist es ein großes Binnengewässer. Meist gibt es keine starke Strömung, dafür aber genug Wind, um Segeltrimm und Manöver zu üben. Wer zum ersten Mal ein Boot chartert, profitiert außerdem von der Infrastruktur rundherum: Marinas mit Service, Einkaufsmöglichkeiten in Hafennähe und gut markierte Fahrwasser. Viele Häfen haben geschützte Boxen, was das Anlegen deutlich entspannter macht.

Praktische Tipps für den Start:

prüfe den Wetterbericht und achte besonders auf Böen

trage beim An- und Ablegen Schwimmwesten und verteile klare Aufgaben

plane eine Route mit Alternativen, damit du flexibel bleiben kannst

Beste Reisezeit für den Segelurlaub am IJsselmeer

Die Saison läuft grob von Mai bis September. Mai, Juni und der frühe September sind oft ruhiger, während Juli und August voller werden. Auch im Sommer kann es auf dem Wasser kühl sein. Nimm deshalb eine winddichte Jacke, rutschfeste Schuhe und einen warmen Midlayer mit. So bleibt der Törn auch bei wechselhaftem Wetter angenehm. Wer früh startet, findet außerdem häufiger freie Liegeplätze.

Hafenstädte und Stopps mit Abwechslung

Am IJsselmeer liegen viele alte Handelsstädte, die sich ideal für einen Landgang eignen. Enkhuizen bietet historisches Hafenflair, Hoorn eine schöne Promenade und Medemblik einen entspannten Zwischenstopp. Wenn du es ruhiger magst, wähle zwischendurch kleinere Marinas. Das ist besonders angenehm für Familien, weil kurze Etappen mehr Zeit an Land lassen.

Für konkrete Ideen wie Wochenendtouren, eine beste Segelroute IJsselmeer oder eine Übersicht zum Yachtcharter IJsselmeer hilft ein kompakter Guide zum Thema segeltörn ijsselmeer.

Zwei einfache Routenideen

3 Tage

Starte in Lemmer, segle nach Enkhuizen, weiter nach Hoorn und dann zurück. Diese Runde ist abwechslungsreich und lässt Spielraum, wenn der Wind dreht.

1 Woche

Erweitere die Route Richtung Markermeer oder plane zusätzliche Hafentage ein. Ein Puffertag ist sehr hilfreich, wenn du Starkwind abwarten willst oder einen Ort länger genießen möchtest.

Segelboot mieten in Lemmer und die Yacht passend wählen

Wenn du Segelboot mieten Lemmer suchst, schau nicht nur auf Länge und Preis. Für Einsteiger ist eine gutmütige Fahrtenyacht wichtig, plus eine verständliche Einweisung. Familien profitieren von Platz im Cockpit und einer praktischen Pantry. Freundesgruppen achten eher auf Kabinen und eine unkomplizierte Aufteilung. Wer sich unsicher fühlt, kann zusätzlich über einen kurzen Segelkurs für Anfänger oder ein Manövertraining nachdenken.

Checkliste vor dem Buchen:

Kojenanzahl und Stauraum passend zur Crew

Sicherheitsausrüstung vollständig und erklärt

Navigation vorhanden und leicht bedienbar

Support erreichbar, falls unterwegs Fragen auftauchen

Mit einer passenden Yacht, realistischen Tageszielen und etwas Flexibilität wird das IJsselmeer schnell zum Revier, in das man gern zurückkehrt.

Your sister-in-law is standing in your kitchen holding her phone up like she’s checking for rain, and she has just asked the one question you cannot answer: “What’s the Wi-Fi password?” You’ve had that router for two years. The card it came with is gone. The guy from the cable company typed something in and left. And the whole time, the phone in your own pocket has been connected to that exact network every day. The password was never lost. Your phone has been holding it for you.

A factory reset does force the router back to the password on the sticker. It also drops every device in your house off the network at once: the smart TV, the thermostat, the printer, the doorbell camera. You reconnect each one by hand, typing that password over and over. Treat the reset as the last move, never the quick one.

Pixel and stock Android: three taps to a code she can scan

If you carry a Google Pixel or any phone running close to plain Android, you can hand the network over without either of you reading a single character out loud. Thirty seconds, start to finish.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Network & internet.
  3. Tap Internet.
  4. Next to your network’s name, tap the gear icon. That opens the Network details screen.
  5. In the row of buttons across the top — Forget, Disconnect, Share — tap Share.
  6. Confirm with your fingerprint, face, PIN, or pattern.
  7. A QR code fills the screen. Hold the phone up and let her camera do the rest.

On a Pixel the password is also printed in plain text on the line directly under that QR code, ready to copy. Treat that line as a bonus. The QR code is the part to count on: Android has built this handoff in since Android 10 arrived in September 2019. Only the name of the button changes from brand to brand.

For a network you’re not connected to right now — the guest network, the cabin Wi-Fi from last summer — go back to Internet, scroll to the bottom, tap Saved networks, pick it, then tap Share. Same gear icon, same button, same QR code — the only difference is that you had to go find the network in the list first.

Samsung Galaxy: different menu, different button, less on screen

Samsung is the exception, and it’s a big one, because Galaxy phones are everywhere. Follow Pixel instructions on a Galaxy and you’ll never find the buttons, decide your phone is broken, and go hunting for a screwdriver. Take this path instead — one minute.

  1. Open Settings.
  2. Tap Connections — “Network & internet” does not exist here.
  3. Tap Wi-Fi.
  4. Tap the gear icon next to your network’s name.
  5. Tap QR code. On Samsung the button is not called “Share.”

You get a QR code and nothing else. Samsung never prints the password in plain text, no matter how many times you tap. If you need the actual characters, take a screenshot of the QR screen, open Google Photos, tap that screenshot, and tap the Lens button at the bottom of the picture. Don’t point a second camera at the glowing screen; it will only offer to connect, not show the text. What comes back reads: WIFI:T:WPA;S:YourNetworkName;P:YourPasswordHere;H:false;; Your password sits after P: and stops at the next semicolon. Labels differ again on Xiaomi and OnePlus, so if neither wording matches, look for the gear beside the network name — the share screen is one tap behind it.

iPhone: the password is in Settings, in plain text, ready to copy

Apple stopped hiding this in iOS 16 and iPadOS 16.1. Ten seconds for the network you’re on right now.

  1. Open Settings and tap Wi-Fi.
  2. Find the network you’re using.
  3. Tap the Info button — the small “i” in a circle to the right of the name.
  4. Tap the Password field.
  5. Authenticate with your passcode, Face ID, or Touch ID. The password appears, with a Copy option.

For a network you’ve used before but aren’t on now, tap Edit in the top right corner of the Wi-Fi screen and authenticate. The full Known Networks list opens. Tap the Info button beside any network with a lock icon, then tap Password.

On iOS 18 or newer there’s a better move for the guest in your kitchen: open the Passwords app, authenticate, tap the Wi-Fi card, pick your network, then tap Copy Password — or tap Show Network QR Code and let an Android guest scan it. No AirDrop, no adding her to your contacts, no reading 20 characters out loud twice.

The catch nobody tells you

Three things quietly break the advice above, and the quick how-tos skip all three.

The iPhone one. Sometimes you tap Password, authenticate correctly, and nothing appears. The explanation you’ll read online — that it came from a different Apple ID — is backwards. Passwords you typed on your own iPad under the same Apple ID do show up: iCloud Keychain carries Wi-Fi credentials across your approved devices. The real cause: if you joined that network by accepting the “Share Password” pop-up from someone else’s iPhone, the password was never written into your keychain. Apple’s proximity sharing hands your phone a finished connection over Bluetooth without storing the text — deliberate protection for whoever owns the network. So the rule that holds is: you only see a password somebody typed by hand on that specific device. The fix, Forget This Network and rejoin by typing it manually, only helps if you already know it. If you don’t, go to the router.

The Samsung one. A Galaxy shares only the network it’s connected to right then. A saved but disconnected network can’t be shared at all.

The sticker one, and this is the one that bites you after two years. What’s printed on that label is the factory default. It’s only still correct if nobody ever changed the password on the admin page — not you, not your son-in-law, not the technician who “fixed the internet” one afternoon. The label keeps advertising the old password with total confidence.

If no phone in the house can help: the router page

This works when every phone fails you, and it costs about five minutes.

The router’s admin password is a different password from your Wi-Fi password. Typing your Wi-Fi password into that login page fails every time and makes people conclude the router is dead. On routers sold since 2020 the old “admin / admin” default is gone. Some units print a random admin password on the same label as the serial number; others make you invent one the first time the setup page opens. Read the label first — if there is no admin password printed on it, the one you need is whatever you or the installer typed on day one.

First get the right address. There’s no universal one, and a wrong guess just times out with no explanation.

Address to type Brands that use it by default 192.168.1.1 ASUS, Netgear, Linksys, Cisco 192.168.0.1 TP-Link, D-Link, Arris, Tenda

To skip the guessing: on Windows, press the Windows key, type cmd, press Enter, type ipconfig, press Enter again, and read the Default Gateway line. On iPhone, tap the Info button in Wi-Fi and read the Router field. On Android, read the Gateway field on the Network details screen. Type that address into your browser, log in with the admin credentials, then open Wireless, Wi-Fi Settings, or Wireless Security. The field you want is labeled Passphrase, Pre-Shared Key (PSK), WPA Pre-Shared Key, or Wi-Fi Password. If it shows dots, click the eye icon or the Reveal checkbox beside it. If the page refuses your login outright, you have a locked gateway from your internet provider. The password lives in the app you already use to pay that bill — Xfinity, My Spectrum, AT&T Smart Home Manager, My Verizon — under the section called Internet or Wi-Fi. Log in there and the network name and password are printed on the screen.

How to know you’ve got the real one

A WPA2 or WPA3 password is between 8 and 63 characters — that’s the wireless standard, not a guideline. If what you found is 6 characters or 70, you’re looking at a serial number, not the password. The only proof that counts takes five seconds: your guest types it in, the spinner stops, a web page loads. Then write it on a card and tape it inside a kitchen cabinet.

Pick up your phone now, before the next visitor asks. Open Settings, follow the path for your brand, and screenshot the password tonight. Thirty seconds today saves you a scavenger hunt behind the TV stand later.

Why Your Phone Got Slow (And the 4 Fixes That Actually Work)

📅 Aug 24, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

You are at the pharmacy counter with the app open, waiting on the barcode. The
screen stays white. The person behind you shifts their weight. Six seconds later the code finally
draws itself. That night you text your daughter and the keyboard runs a full word behind your thumbs
— letters landing after you moved past them.

Your phone is two years old, so you do the math everybody does: worn out, time to spend $900.

It is not worn out. Nothing inside grinds down like brake pads. Four things changed quietly,
and each is a setting you can put back tonight. Take them in this order — most likely first, not
biggest first. Nobody ranks what slows a phone down.

Step zero, iPhone only: the screen that names your symptom

Spend ten seconds on Settings → Battery → Battery
Health, in the section Peak Performance Capability.

As a battery ages, iOS manages the processor to prevent unexpected shutdowns — and Apple names the
visible result as longer app launch times and lower frame rates while scrolling. That is your
pharmacy counter and your lagging keyboard, in the manufacturer’s words. If that screen says
performance management has been applied, your problem is the battery — a fraction of the price
of a new phone.

That screen also offers a Disable option, and it is a one-way door. Apple’s own
wording: if you disable performance management, you can’t turn it back on. It returns only after another
unexpected shutdown. Leave the switch alone.

Fix 1: get back above 20 percent free space

You have heard “never pass 90 percent full.” Neither company publishes that number. Android’s
source code publishes a real one: 20.

Above 20 percent free, Android sets aside 10 percent of your drive as cache room for your apps.
Below the low mark — 5 percent of the drive or 500 MB, whichever is smaller — that reserve drops to
2 percent, and in between the code shrinks it a little for every gigabyte you fill. On a 128 GB phone
that is about 13 GB of cache room while you still have 30 GB free, and about 7.5 GB once you are 90
percent full. Your apps spend the day rebuilding what the system threw out. The chip is not tired — the
operating system is squeezing it.

iPhone: Settings → General → iPhone
Storage, and give it a minute to count. Then set the switch that handles this forever:
Settings → Apps → App Store → Offload
Unused Apps. No fear needed: your iPhone removes the app but keeps its documents and data, the icon stays on your Home Screen with a small cloud, and tapping it reinstalls
the app.

Pixel and stock Android: Settings → Storage →
Free up space → Select files → Move files to
Trash. For autopilot, go back to the Free up space screen, tap
Settings there, and turn on Smart Storage.

Samsung: Settings → Battery and device care →
Storage, tap a category such as Images or Videos,
press and hold one file to start selecting — or tap All at the top of the screen —
then Delete all → Move to Trash. Samsung renames this menu often, so if
those words are missing, tap the magnifying glass in Settings and type
storage.

Fix 2: the apps still working while you are not looking

Every app refreshing itself in the background spends your battery and your data on your behalf,
whether or not you opened it. Trimming the list is a five-minute job.

iPhone: in Settings, under General, find
Background App Refresh and switch it off for the apps you open twice a month. Apple
frames this as a battery-and-data feature, not a speed feature — less competing for your charge.

Pixel and stock Android: Settings → Apps →
See all apps → tap the app → App battery usage → Allow
background usage. Google’s three settings, in its words: Unrestricted allows
all background work and might consume more battery; Optimized, the default, adjusts to how
you use the app; Restricted restricts background battery use, and some apps might not work
as expected and some notifications might be delayed. Restrict a messaging app and your texts arrive late, or never.

Samsung: One UI moves these controls between versions — tap the magnifying glass
in Settings and type background.

Fix 3: cache — Android can dump it, your iPhone cannot

Pixel and stock Android: Settings → Apps →
See all apps → tap the app → Storage & cache → Clear
cache. On Samsung: Settings → Apps → find the app →
Storage → Clear cache. Do your three heaviest apps and you are out in
two minutes. Google’s footnote: this deletes temporary data, and some apps may be slow the next
time you open them. That wears off.

On that identical screen sits a second button — Clear storage on Pixel,
Clear data on Samsung. It is not a stronger version of the same job. Samsung
spells it out: all saved information is permanently deleted — files, settings, accounts, databases.
Tap it on WhatsApp and your chat history is gone.

iPhone: there is no system-wide clear-cache button. The only documented one is Safari’s: Settings → Apps → Safari →
Clear History and Website Data → pick a range → Clear History.
Apple notes it does not change your AutoFill information. For Instagram or TikTok there is no button
at all — deleting and reinstalling is the only route, so know that password first.

Fix 4: the update that has been sitting there since spring

iPhone: Settings → General → Software
Update → Download and Install. If it complains about space, tap
Continue: in Apple’s words, the system removes apps temporarily to make room and
reinstalls them automatically afterward. Then open Automatic Updates. The row
called iOS Updates, under the heading Automatically Install, is the
whole-new-version one. Security patches ride on a separate switch: on most iPhones it is the row named
Security Responses & System Files on that same screen; on the newest iOS it moved
to Settings → Privacy & Security → Background Security
Improvements → Automatically Install. Turn that one on and you get security
fixes without being pushed onto a whole new version. If neither name is on your screen, type
security in the search box at the top of Settings.

Pixel: Settings → System → Software
update. Samsung: Settings → Software
update → Download and install.

In Google’s words, updates roll out gradually and depend on your carrier and device, so it can
take a few weeks to reach your phone. And do not panic if your Galaxy shows an older Android number
than a Pixel — Samsung builds One UI on its own calendar. “Up to date” means newest available
for your phone.

The catch: the “memory cleaner” app is the thing slowing you down

This is why those four fixes did not stick last time.

On Android, cleaner apps cannot do the one thing they advertise. Google closed that door in
Android 14, back in 2023: an app that calls the function for killing background processes can now
only kill its own. Feed it another app’s name and nothing happens. That “1.2 GB freed!” counter is
theater.

Google spells out the damage on the same page: Android keeps apps cached and kills them itself
when it needs memory, and killing them unnecessarily reduces performance and increases
battery drain, because a restart costs far more than resuming a cached app. Then the flat
sentence, in Google’s own documentation — it is not possible for a third-party application to improve
the memory, power, or thermal behavior of an Android device.

To show you that permanent “RAM in use” notification, a cleaner runs a foreground service around
the clock. It is literally the background app that never sleeps — the exact thing it claims to
hunt. Precedent with a name: Clean Master, past a billion downloads, was pulled from the Play Store in
February 2020 — 45 apps from its maker, Cheetah Mobile, went out with it, inside a sweep of nearly
600 apps Google banned for disruptive ads and ad fraud.

On your iPhone, the sandbox stops any app from touching another app’s data, so nothing on the App
Store can clear Instagram’s cache for you. And swiping every app up in the switcher is not
maintenance — Apple says force-quit only when an app is frozen. Craig Federighi, Apple’s software
engineering chief, was asked if he closes apps and if it helps battery life. He answered: “No and no.”

One honest exception: Samsung’s own cleaner at Settings → Battery and
device care → Memory → Clean now is a system app, so it
really does close background apps. Google’s argument still applies, though: the price is a slower
reopen.

How to know it worked: reopen the storage screen and confirm you are above 20
percent free. Then repeat what annoyed you — the app at the counter, one sentence typed to somebody. If the letters keep up with your thumbs, you just saved a purchase.
If that battery screen still says performance management has been applied, book the battery.

Do the storage check tonight, before your phone drops under 20 percent
again — and delete any cleaner app you already installed. We publish

Deleted a Photo by Accident? You Have About 30 Days.

📅 Aug 21, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

You were on the couch thumbing through your camera roll, clearing out eleven near-identical shots of a parking lot, and your thumb went one photo too far. The picture slid off the screen — your father at the kitchen table, the last one anybody ever took of him. Here is what nobody tells you in that moment: on your iPhone, on your Galaxy and in Google Photos, deleting a photo does not erase it. It moves it. It sits in a holding folder for about 30 days, waiting for you. Most people who “lost” a photo forever never opened that folder.

If you learned somewhere that Google Photos holds deleted pictures for 60 days, stop planning around that number. Google’s own support pages now say 30 days — on Android, on iPhone, on the computer, for photos that were backed up and for photos that never were. Open your own trash, read what your app says about time remaining, and plan for 30.

Do these three things first, in this order

Your photo is almost certainly still on your phone right now. What kills it is the next fifteen minutes of well-meant activity: tidying up, freeing space, installing a recovery app that wants your card number and full access to your gallery before it will show you a single thumbnail.

  1. Open the trash inside the app you deleted from — Photos on the iPhone, Google Photos, Samsung Gallery. Each keeps its own.
  2. Open the trash of whatever backs your phone up: Google Photos, iCloud, OneDrive.
  3. Check the computer you plug that phone into. Any photo you once imported into a folder there is a separate copy.
Do not empty any trash today. Not to free space, not because a pop-up suggests it. Emptying the bin is the one action here that cannot be undone — by you, by Apple, by Google, by any app on any store. Google says it plainly: items that are permanently deleted can’t be restored. Leave every bin exactly as it is until your photo is back in your library.

iPhone and iPad: the album is locked, which is why you never found it

Apple keeps your deleted photos in an album called Recently Deleted, and since iOS 16 that album is locked behind Face ID — which is why you scrolled right past it. Two minutes, start to finish.

  1. Open Photos, then tap Collections at the bottom.
  2. Swipe up and find Recently Deleted in the Utilities section. That album is the destination — if a future update shuffles the tabs again, Utilities is the word to hunt for.
  3. Tap View Album, then unlock it with Face ID or Touch ID.
  4. Tap Select, then tap the photo you want to keep.
  5. Tap the More button — the three dots — then tap Recover. To bring everything back at once, tap Recover All.
  6. Tap Recover again to confirm.

Apple’s deadline leaves no room to negotiate: anything in that album stays 30 days, and after 30 days it’s permanently deleted and you can’t get it back. If your iPhone is dead or drowned, the same album lives elsewhere. On a Mac, open Photos and select Recently Deleted in the Utilities section of the sidebar, unlock with Touch ID, click the photo, click Recover. In a browser, sign in at iCloud.com, open Photos, click Recently Deleted in the sidebar and click Recover.

Google Photos: press and hold, don’t tap

This is where most people quit after ten seconds, because tapping a photo in the trash just opens it and nothing useful appears. The gesture is a press and hold.

  1. Open the Google Photos app on your Android phone, your iPhone or your iPad.
  2. At the bottom, tap Collections, then Trash. Some phones label it Bin — same place, same steps.
  3. Touch and hold the photo or video you want back until it’s selected.
  4. At the bottom, tap Restore.

On a computer, go to photos.google.com/login, click Trash on the left, put your cursor over the photo and click Select — the small circle that appears in the photo’s top left corner when you hover — then click Restore at the top right. Your photo goes back to three places at once: the albums it was in, your Google Photos library, and your phone’s gallery app.

Samsung Galaxy: three doors into the same bin

One thing to know before you start, because Samsung’s own instructions disagree with each other depending on how old your phone is: on newer Galaxy phones the recycle bin is simply on, but on some One UI versions it is a switch you had to flip yourself. Don’t guess — open it and look. Ninety seconds.

  1. Open Gallery and tap the Menu — the three horizontal lines.
  2. Tap Recycle bin. Depending on your phone, the label reads Trash or Bin instead.
  3. Tap Edit. On newer Galaxy phones there is no Edit button — just touch and hold the photo until a checkmark appears on it.
  4. Select the items you want to keep, then tap Restore — or Restore All.

If your Gallery doesn’t show that menu, two other doors open the same bin: My Files → Recycle bin → Edit → Restore, or the long road through Settings → Device care → Storage management → Recycle bin. Items stay 30 days, then they’re deleted automatically.

And if there is no Recycle bin anywhere in Gallery, that’s your answer for this photo — it went straight out, so check the Google Photos trash above instead. Then turn the bin on so tonight is the last time this happens: Gallery → Menu → Settings → Recycle bin.

The catch nobody tells you

The clock started when you deleted it, not when you noticed. The countdown is stamped on the file the second it lands in the bin, and a cleanup job does the subtraction. Nothing restarts it when you finally realize what’s missing. If the photo vanished during a cleanup three weeks ago, you don’t have 30 days — you have about 9. Look tonight, not this weekend.

The trash frees up no space at all, and that’s the trap. Deleted photos keep occupying your storage until they expire, so your phone goes right on complaining that it’s full. You search for a fix, you find “empty the trash,” you tap it — and that single tap is the only irreversible move available to you. Worse: if Google Photos shows you a Delete permanently prompt while you’re merely moving pictures to the trash, your trash is already full and those photos are skipping the bin entirely. Zero days of grace.

Deleting on one device deletes it on all of them. The hope everybody has — “it’s probably still on the tablet” — is almost always false. With iCloud Photos on, Apple deletes that photo on every device signed in with the same Apple Account. Google goes further: deleting in the app also removes the copy sitting on your phone, and with backup on, the copy on every other device too.

If it came from a Shared Library, the button may not be yours. Apple only lets the person who added a photo to a Shared Library recover it or delete it for good. If your wife added it and you deleted it, you have to ask her — and those 30 days keep running while you two sort it out. Call her today.

The backup on your computer may hold no photos at all. Plugging your iPhone into the PC and making a backup is not the same as copying your pictures. With iCloud Photos on, your photos live in the sync, and that backup file can be enormous without containing the one you want. What saves you is importing photos as files into a folder — Pictures on Windows, the Photos app or Image Capture on a Mac.

How to know it actually worked

Two checks, thirty seconds. Find the photo in your main library — it returns to its original date, not to the end of the roll, so scroll to the day it was taken, not to today. Then go back into the bin: it should be gone from there. If it’s still sitting in the trash, tap Restore or Recover again. Then open it and use Share to send it somewhere else — a photo that exists in one app only is one thumb-slip from this same afternoon.

Open the trash on your phone right now — Recently Deleted, Trash or Recycle bin — and look at what’s in there. Some of it you meant to delete. Some of it you didn’t, and you’ve spent weeks assuming it was gone. You have about 30 days from the day each one disappeared, and that clock has been running the whole time.

Deshalb haben wir unsere Position zur regulierten Aufbereitung von Einmal-Medizinprodukten in einem Statement zusammengefasst. Darin geht es um die regulatorischen, prozessualen und qualitativen Anforderungen, die für den Einsatz reguliert aufbereiteter Medizinprodukte entscheidend sind und um die Frage, wie diese Anforderungen in der Praxis nachvollziehbar umgesetzt werden können.

Statement zur regulierten Aufbereitung von Einmal-Medizinprodukten

Die Aufbereitung von Einmal-Medizinprodukten wird zunehmend zum Thema in Kliniken, OP-Teams und Einkaufsabteilungen. Das ist gut. Denn wer über Versorgungssicherheit, Wirtschaftlichkeit und Nachhaltigkeit im Gesundheitswesen spricht, kommt an geprüften Kreislaufmodellen nicht vorbei.

Gleichzeitig zeigt sich: Je relevanter Medical Remanufacturing wird, desto häufiger werden pauschale Zweifel formuliert. Es geht dann um angeblich unklare Verantwortlichkeiten, mögliche Leistungsschwankungen, operative Risiken, verdeckte Kosten oder Reputationsfragen. Diese Punkte klingen auf den ersten Blick gewichtig. Entscheidend ist jedoch, ob sie belegt sind.

Einmal behauptet ist nicht halb bewiesen.

Medical Remanufacturing ist weder ein Experiment noch ein rechtsfreier Raum, sondern unterliegt klaren regulatorischen Anforderungen. In der EU bildet die Medizinprodukteverordnung (MDR) den rechtlichen Rahmen. Es gelten die Vorgaben aus Artikel 17 MDR, je nach Aufbereitungsmodell sind entweder alle Herstellerpflichten der MDR zu erfüllen oder Herstellerpflichten der MDR zusammen mit den Anforderungen der Common Specifications des Artikels 17. Diese umfassen unter anderem Risikomanagement, Validierung der Verfahren, Qualitätsmanagement, Produktfreigabe, Leistungsprüfung, Dokumentation, Rückverfolgbarkeit und Vorkommnismeldung.

Im Vereinigten Königreich erfolgt Medical Remanufacturing im Einklang mit der UK Guidance 2016 in der jeweils gültigen Fassung. Dabei muss der „Re-Manufacturer" alle Vorgaben der relevanten Medizinprodukteverordnung erfüllen. Gemäß der UK Guidance 2016 wird vor dem Inverkehrbringen oder der Inbetriebnahme das Medizinprodukt gereinigt, desinfiziert und sterilisiert sowie gemäß den Spezifikationen des Re-Manufacturers geprüft, um sicherzustellen, dass das Einmalprodukt weiterhin sicher und bestimmungsgemäß funktioniert. Das Einmal-Medizinprodukt muss nach erfolgtem Medical Remanufacturing erneut mit einer Konformitätskennzeichnung (CE-Zeichen oder UKCA-Zeichen) versehen werden, für das der Re-Manufacturer verantwortlich ist. Voraussetzung dafür ist bei allen steril zur Anwendung kommenden Medizinprodukten eine Zertifizierung der Konformität durch eine Benannte Stelle bzw. einem UK Approved Body.

Genau deshalb lohnt sich ein genauer Blick auf die Punkte, die in der Diskussion immer wieder genannt werden:

Verantwortung und Rückverfolgbarkeit

Die Behauptung, bei reguliert aufbereiteten Einmal-Medizinprodukten könne unklar werden, wer im Falle eines Vorkommnisses verantwortlich ist, greift zu kurz. Das Gegenteil ist der Fall: Verantwortung ist regulatorisch definiert. Die regulatorischen Vorgaben, ob MDR oder UK Guidance, regeln ausdrücklich, unter welchen Voraussetzungen Einmalprodukte aufbereitet und wiederverwendet werden dürfen. Bei der CE-Aufbereitung übernimmt der Aufbereiter die entsprechenden Herstellerpflichten. Bei der Aufbereitung für Gesundheitseinrichtungen nach Artikel 17 Absatz 3 bis 5 MDR gelten die Common Specifications mit klaren Anforderungen an Dokumentation, Rückverfolgbarkeit, Produktfreigabe und Meldewege. Verantwortung wird dadurch nicht verwischt, sondern strukturiert, dokumentiert und überprüfbar gemacht.

Zuverlässigkeit und gleichbleibende Qualität

Die pauschale Annahme, reguliert aufbereitete Medizinprodukte seien weniger zuverlässig oder weniger konsistent als Original-Einmalprodukte, ist keine belastbare fachliche Bewertung. Entscheidend ist nicht, ob ein Produkt ursprünglich als Einmalprodukt in Verkehr gebracht wurde, sondern ob seine Aufbereitung validiert, qualitätsgesichert und regulatorisch zulässig erfolgt. Medical Remanufacturing basiert auf zertifizierten Desinfektions-, Reinigungs-, und Prüf- Sterilisationsprozessen. Hinzu kommen Leistungs- und Funktionsprüfungen sowie eine finale Freigabe. Ein Produkt gelangt nur dann wieder in den Einsatz, wenn die festgelegten Anforderungen erfüllt sind.

Abläufe im OP und Unterstützung der Teams

OP-Teams brauchen Produkte, auf die sie sich verlassen können. Genau deshalb ist die regulierte Aufbereitung nicht nur ein technischer, sondern ein qualitätsgesicherter Gesamtprozess. Dazu gehören definierte Annahmekriterien, validierte Aufbereitungsschritte, dokumentierte Prüfungen und klare Freigabekriterien. Reguliert aufbereitete Medizinprodukte sind darauf ausgelegt, sicher und zweckbestimmt in bestehende klinische Abläufe integriert zu werden. Wer mögliche Störungen im OP pauschal mit Aufbereitung verknüpft, sollte konkret belegen, worauf diese Annahme basiert. Eine hypothetische Verunsicherung ersetzt keine faktenbasierte Bewertung.

Wirtschaftlichkeit über den Stückpreis hinaus

Es ist richtig, Medical Remanufacturing nicht allein über den Einzelpreis zu betrachten. Genau das macht Vanguard nicht. Relevant ist die Gesamtbetrachtung aus Qualität, Verfügbarkeit, Prozesssicherheit, Ressourceneinsatz, Abfallvermeidung und wirtschaftlichem Nutzen. Wer jedoch suggeriert, Einsparungen würden durch angebliche Leistungsschwankungen, zusätzlichen Produktverbrauch oder höheren Personalaufwand regelmäßig aufgehoben, muss diese Behauptung belegen. Regulierte Aufbereitung ist kein reines Sparmodell. Sie verbindet wirtschaftliche Entlastung mit qualitätsgesicherten Prozessen und einem verantwortungsvolleren Umgang mit Ressourcen.

Patiententransparenz und Kommunikation

Transparenz gegenüber Patientinnen und Patienten ist wichtig. Sie darf jedoch nicht mit unbegründeten Bedenken gleichgesetzt werden. Reguliert aufbereitete Medizinprodukte sind keine Produkte zweiter Klasse. Sie dürfen nur dann erneut eingesetzt werden, wenn die regulatorischen Anforderungen erfüllt sind. Eine sachliche Kommunikation kann erklären, dass Medical Remanufacturing auf regulatorischen Vorgaben, validierten Prozessen und dokumentierter Qualitätssicherung basiert. Gerade in einem privaten Behandlungsumfeld ist eine transparente, verständliche und faktenbasierte Einordnung entscheidend. Nicht die Verwendung reguliert aufbereiteter Produkte ist erklärungsbedürftig, sondern jede pauschale Abwertung ohne Nachweis.

Reputation und Verantwortung der Klinik

Der Einsatz geprüfter Kreislaufmodelle kann ein starkes Zeichen verantwortungsvoller Klinikführung sein. Krankenhäuser stehen heute vor der Aufgabe, hohe Versorgungsqualität, wirtschaftlichen Druck, Ressourcenknappheit und Nachhaltigkeitsziele miteinander zu verbinden. Regulierte Aufbereitung bietet dafür einen rechtlich vorgesehenen und qualitätsgesicherten Weg. Reputationsrisiken entstehen nicht dadurch, dass Kliniken geprüfte, sichere und nachhaltigere Lösungen einsetzen. Sie entstehen eher dort, wo Entscheidungen auf unbelegten Annahmen, einseitigen Interessen oder pauschaler Verunsicherung beruhen.

Wir nehmen wahr, dass Medical Remanufacturing inzwischen auch von etablierten Marktteilnehmern sehr genau beobachtet wird. Das zeigt, dass das Thema im Markt angekommen ist. Eine sachliche Diskussion wird jedoch erschwert, wenn pauschale Behauptungen ohne belastbaren Nachweis im Raum stehen. Wer Fragen zur regulierten Aufbereitung hat, sollte sie fachlich klären, nicht durch Verunsicherung ersetzen.

Für Krankenhäuser sollte im Mittelpunkt stehen, welche Lösung Sicherheit, Qualität, Wirtschaftlichkeit und Nachhaltigkeit bestmöglich verbindet. Der reine Vergleich von Stückpreisen greift dabei ebenso zu kurz wie die pauschale Abwertung aufbereiteter Medizinprodukte. Entscheidend ist die Qualität des Prozesses – von der Annahme über die Reinigung, Prüfung und Dokumentation bis zur finalen Freigabe.

Medical Remanufacturing ist kein Kompromiss, sondern ein regulierter Beitrag, der Patientensicherheit, Ressourcenschonung und Wirtschaftlichkeit miteinander verbindet. Damit trägt es zu einem nachhaltigeren und wirtschaftlicheren Gesundheitswesen bei. Wer darüber diskutiert, sollte dies auf Basis von Fakten tun.

Durch den heißen Sommer sind die Wiener Bäder auch 2026 wieder sehr gut besucht. Wie die Strand- und Freibäder in den österreichischen Medien abschneiden, hat die Brand Intelligence Agentur »OBSERVER« in einer Medienanalyse untersucht. Analysiert wurde die Berichterstattung zu 24 Wiener Bädern im Zeitraum von 2. Mai bis 17. August 2026. Das Ergebnis: Bei Medienpräsenz und Tonalität gibt es deutliche Unterschiede. Your Phone Says “Storage Full.” Here Is What Is Actually Taking the Space.

Your granddaughter is leaning over the cake, the candles are lit, everybody is
singing, and you raise your phone. Instead of the shutter sound you get a gray box:
Storage Almost Full. You tap OK, you try again, you get the same
box. By the time you give up, the candles are out and you have nothing.

So that night you do what everybody does. You open Photos and start deleting —
the 2019 beach trip, the screenshots of your daughter’s flight number, the twelve near-identical
shots of the dog. You get a little room back. Thursday the box returns. Now you are down photos you
cannot get back, and the phone is still full.

Here is what almost nobody uses: your phone already counted everything and sorted it biggest
first, on one screen. Read it and you stop guessing — because your problem pile may not be your
neighbor’s.

Step zero: stop guessing and read the bar

On iPhone: open Settings → General →
iPhone Storage, and give it about a minute to finish counting.

The colored bar breaks your phone into Apps, Photos, Messages and System Data
(the category once called “Other”). Under it, Apple lists every app sorted by size,
largest first — the honest answer for your phone. Above it you may see
Recommendations with a Show All link.

On Android: open Settings → Storage, then tap a
category to see inside it. On a Samsung Galaxy it lives under Settings →
Battery and device care → Storage; on the newer Galaxy phones that
same menu reads only Device care, with Battery broken out as its
own line above it. Google itself says settings
vary by phone: if neither path matches, type Storage into the search box at the top of
Settings. That works on any brand.

Now start where your phone tells you to start, not where an article tells you to.

Hiding place 1: the media WhatsApp saved for you

Every video your cousin forwards to the family group lands on your phone as a file. Not a link — a

  1. In WhatsApp, open Settings → Storage and data (on some
    phones it reads Storage and mobile data) → Manage storage.
  2. At the top you get two shortcuts: Larger than 5 MB and
    Forwarded many times. Tap Larger than 5 MB.
  3. That list already arrives with the heaviest file on top — you do not have to sort anything.
    Tap Select in the top corner (on iPhone the same button reads
    Manage), tap Select All, tap the trash can and confirm. This
    removes the files and keeps the written messages.

On Android there is a faster door. Open Files by Google →
Clean → the card that says Delete WhatsApp media →
Select files → tick what you want gone → at the bottom tap the blue bar that reads
Move X files to Trash, and confirm. Then finish the job in the same app: the menu
(three lines, top left) → Trash → delete what is sitting in there. Google built a
card for one app’s leftovers. That tells you how common this is.

Hiding place 2: app cache (Android only — and this matters)

Cache is temporary junk an app keeps to load faster. On Android you dump it in five seconds per
app: Settings → Apps → the app →
Storage & cache → Clear cache. On Samsung:
Settings → Apps → the app → Storage →
Clear cache. Nothing you care about is lost.

Right beside it sits Clear storage. Google’s own wording: Clear cache
deletes temporary data, Clear storage permanently deletes all app data. Tap
Clear storage on WhatsApp and your chat history is gone — not hidden, gone.
Read the button before your thumb lands.

iPhone owners: skip this step. iOS has no clear-cache button. Not hidden — it
does not exist. Any tutorial that sends you looking for it is describing a different phone.

Hiding place 3: the attachments buried in your texts (iPhone)

Back in iPhone Storage, look under Recommendations for
Review Large Attachments. It lists every photo, video and PDF ever sent to you in
Messages, biggest first. Delete from the top.

Then close the tap so it does not refill: Settings → Apps →
Messages → Keep Messages. Three choices —
30 Days, 1 Year, Forever — and your phone
shipped on Forever. On an older iPhone there is no Apps list in
Settings; tap Messages straight down the Settings list instead,
and Keep Messages is in the same place inside it.

Hiding place 4: downloads you forgot you downloaded

The insurance PDF, the boarding pass, the coffee maker manual. Two minutes:

iPhone: open Files → On My iPhone →
Downloads. Tap the three dots (…) in the top right corner: the
sort choices — Name, Kind, Date,
Size, Tags — sit right there on some iPhones and one level in,
under View Options, on others. Either way you want Size. The
heaviest file jumps to the top; delete from the top. If you download episodes
in the TV app, Recommendations may also offer Review Downloaded
Videos — one movie can outweigh a thousand photos.

Android: Settings → Storage →
Free up space — on most phones that button opens the cleanup screen of
Files by Google. Scroll to the card named Downloaded files, tap
Select files, then Move X files to Trash — and empty that Trash
afterwards from the menu, or the files are still on the phone. To keep it tidy on its own, reopen
that screen, tap the menu
→ Settings → turn on Smart Storage.

Hiding place 5: apps you have not opened in months

iPhone: in iPhone Storage, tap any app and you get
Last Used plus two buttons. Offload App removes the program and
keeps your documents and data — the icon stays, with a small cloud on it.
Delete App removes everything. After a big iOS update, people have reported
Last Used resetting to the update date, so if a date looks wrong, go by size
instead: the heavy app you do not recognize. Automate it at
Settings → Apps → App Store →
Offload Unused Apps.

Android: open the Play Store → your profile photo →
Manage apps & device → Manage → the
Unused apps filter, which shows how long each one has sat untouched. To automate:
profile photo → Settings → General →
Automatically archive apps. It ships turned off, it only acts once the phone is
already tight, and an archived app keeps your data and gets a cloud icon.

The catch: deleting the conversation does not delete the files

This is where most people lose an evening. You delete the loud group chat, you check the bar, and
almost nothing has changed.

On Android, the media WhatsApp downloaded does not live inside the conversation.
It sits in a separate folder — Internal storage → Android → media → com.whatsapp → WhatsApp →
Media. Deleting the chat removes the message; the files stay there, still taking the space.
They only go with the chat if you check the box that reads Also delete media received in
this chat from device gallery. Miss that box and you now have neither the messages nor the room.

On iPhone the leak works differently. There is no folder to browse, but if
WhatsApp has Save to Camera Roll on, every photo and video you received was
copied into Photos. Two files, two places. Deleting the chat never touches
the copy — so on iPhone you clean both, then empty Recently Deleted.

And the part that makes people give up: if the same 80 MB video reached you in three groups,
there are three copies, and deleting one frees one. That is why you delete something big and watch
the number barely move.

Only now: your photos

iPhone: open Photos → tap Collections at the
bottom → swipe up to the section called Utilities → Recently
Deleted → unlock with Face ID → Select → Delete All. If
you do not see Utilities at all, scroll to the very bottom, tap
Customize & Reorder and tick it. Anything you
“deleted” sits there for 30 days, still using your storage, unless you empty it yourself. If you
use iCloud, also turn on Settings → your name → iCloud →
Photos → Optimize iPhone Storage.

Android: open Google Photos → your profile photo →
Free up space on this device → confirm on the button that reads Free up X
from device. That deletes only the local copy of what is already backed up. Then tap
Collections at the bottom (older versions of the app call that tab
Library) → Trash → Empty trash: the trash holds
backed-up items 60 days and never-backed-up items 30. What looks like a bug and is not — Google says
pictures under 30 days old may be left on the phone on purpose, so if most of yours are recent you
get little back.

bar recount. The gigabytes used should have dropped and the name that sat at the top should have
moved down. Then open the camera and photograph the wall. If the shutter fires, you are done.

Bei der Einrichtung mit historischen Möbeln ist es wichtig, einen roten Faden zu finden. How to Stop Spam Calls (What Works, What Is a Waste of Time)

📅 Aug 17, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

It’s 11:40 on a Tuesday and your phone lights up for the third time before lunch. The area code is yours. So are the next three digits, which is why your thumb keeps hovering. This time you answer. You say hello, get half a second of dead air — that little hole of silence — then a recording about your car’s extended warranty telling you to press 9 to be removed from the list. Don’t. That one key is the difference between a phone that rings all day and one that doesn’t.

The catch: “press 9 to be removed” is not an opt-out

The FCC says it flatly on its caller ID spoofing page: if a caller or a recording asks you to hit a button to stop the calls, just hang up. The agency’s word for it is a “trick,” used “to identify potential targets.” You aren’t canceling anything. You’re answering the one question the caller wanted answered: this number rings, a human picks it up, and that human follows spoken instructions. The FTC says the same in its National Do Not Call Registry FAQ: pressing a key to get off a list “might lead to more unwanted calls.”

How many more, no agency will say — both stick to might and could, so anyone quoting a percentage invented it. People push back here, and they’re half right: a legitimate company running a recorded sales call must offer an opt-out at the start, and that one is real. The problem is the two seconds you get to tell it from the criminal one. Use the FTC’s test instead of your gut — if it’s a recording, it’s selling something, and you never gave that company written permission to call you that way, the call was illegal before you touched a key. Hang up. Press nothing. Don’t answer their questions, especially anything answerable with yes or no.

What works, step one: 60 seconds on the Do Not Call Registry

  1. Go to DoNotCall.gov and click Register Your Phone.
  2. Enter up to three numbers at a time — the online limit.
  3. Watch your inbox. Each number gets its own email with a link, and you have 72 hours to click it or the registration never completes. Miss the window and you start over from step one.
  4. Or call 1-888-382-1222 (TTY: 1-866-290-4236) from the phone you want to register. Free either way.

Your number lands on the Registry the next day, but the FTC gives sales calls up to 31 days to stop — mark the calendar 31 days out from the day you register and judge it then, not tomorrow. Registration never expires. Know what free bought you: it makes calling you illegal for real telemarketing companies, with fines the FTC puts at up to $50,120 per call, and does nothing to a criminal already breaking the law. A legal telemarketer can’t dial before 8 a.m. or after 9 p.m. your local time, and must reach a live person within two seconds of your hello. That hole of silence at 11:40 is a machine dialing more numbers than it has people to cover.

Step two: five minutes turning on the screening your phone already has

iPhone on iOS 26

  1. Open Settings, tap Apps, tap Phone.
  2. Scroll to Screen Unknown Callers and pick one of three: Never, Ask Reason for Calling (your iPhone asks the caller for more information before it rings), or Silence (silenced, sent to voicemail, still in Recents).
  3. On the same screen, open Call Filtering and turn on Unknown Callers and Spam.
  4. Open Call Blocking & Identification and turn on Business Call Identification, so real companies show a name, not a stranger’s number.

To see what got caught, open Phone → Calls (or Recents), tap Filter at the top right and choose Unknown Callers or Spam. To rescue a real call, tap Mark as Known beside it, then Mark as Known again. If Screen Unknown Callers isn’t there, your language, region or iOS version doesn’t support it — use the iOS 18 path below.

iPhone on iOS 18

Open Settings → Apps → Phone and turn on Silence Unknown Callers; you’re still notified for your contacts, recent outgoing calls and Siri Suggestions. Then open Call Blocking & Identification and turn on Silence Junk Callers (certain carriers only) — the same protection later renamed Spam, so don’t hunt for newer wording on an older phone.

Android, Pixel and Samsung Galaxy

In the Phone app, tap More options (the three dots), then Settings, then Caller ID and spam — on some phones that menu reads Spam and Call Screen. Turn on See caller ID & spam, then Filter spam calls. On a Pixel with automatic Call Screen there is no Filter spam calls switch at all — don’t hunt for it, use the Pixel steps below instead. The tradeoff Google names: no missed-call notification and no voicemail notification, but the call stays in your call history and the voicemail is still there when you open the mailbox.

On a Pixel, use Call Screen and pick Maximum protection, Medium protection or Basic protection; on a Pixel 7 or newer, turn on Automatically screen calls for Spam, Possibly faked numbers and First-time callers. Automatic screening is Pixel-only — on other Androids you tap Screen call yourself while it rings. On a Samsung Galaxy the path is Phone → three dots → Settings → Caller ID and spam protection, then the switch at the top, then Block all spam and scam calls or Only block high-risk scam calls. Some Galaxy owners open that Settings screen and the entry isn’t there — it depends on your model and carrier. If it’s missing, use Phone → three dots → Settings → Block numbers instead, and tap Block number on the call screen each time a junk call comes in.

Step three: the free tool your carrier is already holding

What backfires, and the ten minutes that fix it

Every filter above will eventually silence a call you wanted. Verizon warns its spam filter “may inadvertently block wanted calls”; T-Mobile warns Scam Block “might block calls you want.” In real life: the pharmacy about your refill, or the school nurse dialing from a line you never saved.

Apple gives the fix and almost nobody does it: put the numbers you can’t afford to miss into Contacts — family, your child’s school, your doctor, your pharmacy. Screening reads that list to decide what rings. Ten minutes tonight is the difference between keeping this setup and ripping it out next week.

Three traps. Call 911 from your iPhone and screening switches off for 24 hours. Neither Apple nor Google screens while you’re roaming, whatever your settings say. And silenced calls land in voicemail, so open that mailbox once a week or it fills with junk and stops taking real messages.

What’s a waste of your time

Pressing any key a recording offers. Nine, two, zero, “press 1 for a representative.”

Answering their questions. The FCC is blunt: don’t respond, especially to anything answerable with yes or no. If you do pick up, hang up immediately.

Calling the number back, or blocking numbers one at a time forever. That number was borrowed for the afternoon; tomorrow’s arrives from a different one that looks just as local, and the FTC notes your phone may cap how many the block list holds. Block the repeat offenders, then stop.

Paying before you’ve used the free tier. All three major carriers give it away. Run free 31 days, then decide.

What isn’t a waste: reporting. No money lost — DoNotCall.gov, under Report Unwanted Calls. Money lost — ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Bring the caller ID number even if it’s fake.

How to know it worked

Go to DoNotCall.gov and click Verify Your Registration — your number is either listed or it isn’t. Then open Filter in your Phone app and count what got caught instead of ringing through. The real proof arrives around day 31: a Tuesday morning where your phone sits quiet and the junk piles up in a list you check when you feel like it.

Do the free minute now. Open DoNotCall.gov, click Register Your Phone, click the email link within 72 hours. Then turn the screening on and add your doctor, your pharmacy and your kid’s school to Contacts. And the next time a recording offers you 9, you know what it’s really for.

How to Put Your Phone Screen on Your TV (Every Combination, in Order)

📅 Aug 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

You came home with 40 photos worth showing, and now five people are leaning over one shoulder to squint at a six-inch screen. One of them holds her reading glasses out like a magnifying lens. Somebody keeps saying “go back — the one with the boat.” Eight feet away, a 55-inch screen sits doing nothing. Which of the five paths below is yours comes down to your phone and your TV. Find your row and skip the rest.

Your phone What’s on your TV Your path iPhone Apple TV box Path 1 iPhone Samsung, LG, Sony, Vizio Path 2 Android Chromecast or Google TV Path 3 Android Samsung or LG Path 4 Any phone Old TV, weak Wi-Fi, hotel Path 5

Path 1 — iPhone to an Apple TV

First, put your iPhone on the same Wi-Fi network as your Apple TV — the step everyone skips.

  1. Open Control Center. iPhone X or newer: swipe down from the upper-right corner. iPhone 8 or older: swipe up from the bottom edge.
  2. Tap Screen Mirroring. If it isn’t there, tap the + in the upper-left corner, tap Add a Control, and add Screen Mirroring.
  3. Pick your Apple TV from the list.
  4. If an AirPlay passcode appears on the TV, type those digits on your phone.
  5. To stop: Control Center → Screen Mirroring → Stop Mirroring.

For the 40 photos, skip mirroring entirely. Open Photos, select them, tap Share (the square with the arrow out the top), then tap AirPlay and pick your TV. That sends the picture and nothing else, so the rest of your camera roll stays private. To advance hands-free, select the photos, tap the three dots (More), and tap Slideshow.

Mirroring puts every notification on the wall too — a text from your doctor’s office, a bank alert, a message about somebody in the room. Five people read it the instant you do, in 40-point type, and you cannot un-show it. Turn on Do Not Disturb first.

Path 2 — iPhone to a Samsung or LG, no box needed

Apple builds AirPlay 2 into Samsung, LG, Sony and Vizio sets. Flip one switch tonight, no box required.

Samsung. Press Home, open Settings (All Settings on some sets), then follow your model year. Sets from 2022 through 2026: Connection → Apple AirPlay Settings → AirPlay on. Sets from 2018 through 2021: General → Apple AirPlay Settings. That one word — General versus Connection — is why so many guides send people in circles.

LG. Press Home and open Home Dashboard — the same door reads Home Hub or ThinQ Home Dashboard on other models, and all three land in the same place — then AirPlay → AirPlay and HomeKit Settings → AirPlay on. LG’s whole 2026 line — G6, C6, B6 and W6 — ships with AirPlay 2 inside. Then the phone side matches Path 1.

Path 3 — Android to a Chromecast or Google TV

Google requires three things at once: Android 8.0 or later for the Google Home cast controls, the same wireless network name on both devices, and the two within 15 feet (4 meters) of each other. Check the distance first — moving to the couch fixes it.

Swipe down from the top and tap the cast tile, labeled Cast, Screen Cast, Smart View or Wireless Projection depending on your brand, then pick your TV. No tile? Open Google Home, tap your TV in the device list, then tap Cast my screen and confirm with Cast screen. Android then warns that it is about to capture everything on your screen — tap Start now. To stop, expand the cast notification and tap Disconnect.

For photos, beat mirroring again: in Google Photos, open the album, tap More (three dots), tap Cast, pick your TV, then swipe from one photo to the next — those are Google’s own steps, and only the photo you are on reaches the screen. Works from an iPhone too.

Path 4 — Android to a Samsung or LG

Samsung calls it Smart View, and the official steps are three lines long.

  1. Swipe down from the top right to open Quick settings and tap Smart View.
  2. Select your TV under Available devices and tap Start now. Tap Continue if asked, and enter a PIN if prompted.
  3. Your screen appears on the TV. To disconnect, open Quick settings and tap the Smart View icon.

Samsung’s rule is flat: devices must be on the same Wi-Fi network as the TV. This year, Samsung’s 2026 sets ship with Google Cast built in, and select 2023 and 2024 models (the S90D OLED is confirmed) are getting it by firmware update, model by model. Before you count on it, update the TV and look for a Google Cast entry in its settings menu. Whichever door lists your TV is the right one. On LG, press Home, open Screen Share, then tap the cast tile on your phone.

Path 5 — the cable, when the network won’t cooperate

Nothing here touches Wi-Fi, which is why it works in a hotel room.

iPhone 14 or older (Lightning): Apple’s Lightning Digital AV Adapter, around $50, mirrors at up to 1080p and covers iPhone 5 through 14. iPhone 15 or newer (USB-C): Apple’s USB-C Digital AV Multiport Adapter, model MW5M3AM/A, around $70, with an HDMI port good for up to 4K at 60Hz and compatibility through iPhone 17 Pro — that 4K figure describes the port, not your mirrored screen. Its USB-C port charges the phone while it plays.

Android over HDMI is not universal, and this is where money gets wasted. It works only if your phone has DisplayPort Alt Mode over USB-C: the Pixel 8 and later have it, the Galaxy A54 and A55 do not. You also need a cable that actually carries video, and the speed number on the package does not tell you: a 10 Gbps cable sold for charging can still refuse to send a picture. Buy one whose box says DisplayPort Alt Mode, DP Alt Mode or USB-C video. The charging cable in your drawer will not carry video. Check your model before you buy.

Then switch the TV’s input to that HDMI port — the step people forget while staring at a black screen.

The catch nobody tells you

Your TV doesn’t show up. No error, no message — just an empty list. Nine times out of ten it’s this: both devices must be on the same network, and a router broadcasting 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz under two separate names counts as two networks. Google says it in two separate sentences: same network name (SSID), and same band. Check the band, not just the name.

Discovery uses mDNS, a broadcast that travels only inside one network. Split bands are separate broadcast domains — the same Wi-Fi to you, two neighborhoods to the packet. And the defaults pull your devices apart: phones prefer 5 GHz, a Chromecast falls back to 2.4 GHz on a weak signal.

Four cousins produce the same empty list:

  1. The guest network is a third network. With five people in the room, odds are good somebody’s phone is on it.
  2. AP isolation (client isolation) is on in the router. Same name, same band, still invisible. Google says it must be off.
  3. A VPN is running. It swallows the mDNS broadcast and empties the list. Turn it off, then pull down to refresh.
  4. Smart Connect, Band Steering or Single SSID in the router — why a network looks like one name and behaves like two.

The check takes ten seconds: read the network name in your phone’s Wi-Fi settings, read the name your TV reports, compare them character by character. A trailing “-5G” is a different network.

Two honest exceptions: an Apple TV (3rd generation Rev A, model A1469, or later) can find your iPhone over Bluetooth with no shared network, and Smart View rides Miracast, which also connects directly. Both companies still print “same Wi-Fi network,” so follow that.

One rival cause fits your exact situation. On the Apple TV, open Settings → AirPlay and HomeKit → Allow Access. Set to Only People Sharing This Home, a visitor’s phone never appears no matter how right the network is — and the homeowner swears the Wi-Fi is fine, because his own phone works. Choose Anyone on the Same Network, and check the Require Password toggle.

You know it worked when the TV stops showing its own home screen and starts showing yours — your wallpaper, your clock, your battery icon. iPhone adds a blue Screen Mirroring badge in Control Center; Android leaves a cast notification in the drawer.

Don’t test this for the first time with five people on your couch. Do it tonight: turn AirPlay on or tap the cast tile, get your TV to appear once, and confirm both devices read the same network name letter for letter. Ten quiet minutes now beats twenty minutes of everyone watching you tap.

How to Make a Slow Laptop Usable Again (Free, in About 20 Minutes)

📅 Aug 10, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

You press the power button, walk to the kitchen, pour your coffee, come back —
and your laptop is still on the spinning dots. Double-clicking Chrome buys you time to read the
mail. Everybody says the same thing: buy a new one. Not yet. Almost everything that makes a
4-year-old laptop feel dead is free to undo, and the clicking takes about 20 minutes.

First, decide which kind of slow you have

Two problems hide behind “it got slow,” and they take opposite fixes. Watch your machine for 30
seconds and pick your row, or you will tune something that was never the bottleneck.

What you see What is choking Start at 3 minutes from power button to a usable desktop Programs launching themselves at login, on a full drive Steps 1 and 2 Desktop is fast, then a program takes 3 minutes Memory, and your browser is holding it Steps 4 and 1

Step 1: Shut off the programs that open themselves

While you stare at that circle, 10 to 20 programs are loading ahead of you: the printer helper,
the updater for something you open twice a year, the chat app you signed into once. Your desktop
waits last in line. Five minutes.

Windows 11: press Ctrl + Shift + Esc for Task Manager, then the
Startup apps tab. Click the Startup impact column header to
sort. Every line reads High impact, Medium impact,
Low impact, None or Not measured. Select each
High impact app you do not need the second you log in and press Disable.

Mac: Apple menu → System Settings
→ General → Login Items & Extensions. Under
Open at Login, click the app, then the − button. The
section below it holds the ones that run all day: App Background Activity on
macOS 26 Tahoe, Allow in the Background on macOS 15 Sequoia and earlier.

Do not switch off your backup. OneDrive, iCloud Drive, Dropbox and your
antivirus sit in that list and look like clutter. Turn one off and it stops quietly — no error,
no warning — and you find out on the day you need a file that was never copied.

Step 2: Give your drive room to breathe

A nearly full drive drags everything, and it is the top reason updates fail. Microsoft asks for
6 GB – 11 GB or more of free space for a feature update and
2 GB – 3 GB or more for a monthly one, and Apple’s slow-Mac page also lists low
free space first. Budget 8 minutes.

Windows 11: Start → Settings →
System → Storage. Turn on Storage Sense
so it keeps cleaning by itself, then open Cleanup recommendations on that same
screen. Four groups appear — Temporary files,
Large or unused files, Files synced to the cloud and
Unused apps — each showing how many gigabytes it frees before you agree. Tick and
press Clean up.

Mac: Apple menu → System Settings
→ General → Storage →
Recommendations: Store in iCloud,
Optimize Storage, Empty Trash automatically. Apple publishes no
hard number, but macOS 26 Tahoe upgrades have needed roughly 35 GB to 60 GB of free space depending
on the version you are coming from — keep 40 GB free and the update will not stop
on you.

Read the list before you press Clean up. Temporary files are junk.
Large or unused files is not — it can include your own documents, and clearing
it is a delete with no undo. Empty Trash automatically does the same on a
timer: anything that has sat in your Trash for 30 days is deleted for good.

Step 3: Finish the update that has been half-done for weeks

A machine stuck mid-update retries all day, spending your drive and processor on a job that
never lands — you feel it as a laptop that is busy while you are not. Plug in the charger and climb
this ladder, stopping the moment it goes through. About five minutes of clicking; the rest installs
on its own.

  1. Start → Settings →
    Windows Update → Check for updates. Install,
    restart.
  2. Still failing? Settings → System →
    Troubleshoot → Other troubleshooters, find
    Windows Update, press Run. It hands you to the
    Get Help app — normal, not a broken button.
  3. Still failing? Settings → System →
    Recovery, and under Fix problems using Windows Update press
    Reinstall now. It lays the same Windows over itself and, per Microsoft, leaves
    your files, settings and apps alone.

On a Mac: System Settings → General
→ Software Update, then Update Now,
Upgrade Now or Restart Now, depending on what is waiting. It
asks for your administrator password first. If it swears you are current and the Mac still crawls,
press Check for Update, or hold Command and press R to force a fresh look.

If your Settings app has a section called “Update & Security,” read this

That wording means Windows 10, and plenty of 2022 laptops still run it. Consumer Extended
Security Updates end on October 13, 2026 — about five weeks from now. And if you never enrolled in
that free extension, your patches already stopped on October 14, 2025. Your laptop keeps working
either way and stops getting security patches — which matters more than every speed tip here. Open Windows
Update on that screen and see whether the free Windows 11 upgrade is offered to you. That
decides your evening.

Step 4: Your browser is probably the real hog

If the desktop arrives fast and then programs crawl, this is your section. Those 42 tabs are not
waiting politely. Each holds memory, and when memory runs out your laptop shuffles data onto the
drive — that shuffle is the gap between your fingers and the screen. Three minutes.

Chrome (Windows and Mac): Settings →
Performance. Turn on Memory Saver and pick
Moderate, Balanced (recommended) or Maximum;
Chrome sleeps unused tabs and wakes them when you click back. Keep your webmail and your bank under
Always keep these sites active.

Edge: Settings and more (…) →
Settings → System and performance. Switch on
Save resources with sleeping tabs, then set
Put inactive tabs to sleep after the specified amount of time to
30 minutes. The battery control there reads
Energy saver — Efficiency mode in older versions.

Safari: no memory saver and no tab-sleeping switch. Closing tabs is your only
lever, and any article telling you to turn memory saving on in Safari is describing a button that
does not exist.

The part every other article gets wrong

The honest version: exactly one part swap transforms an old laptop — a mechanical hard drive
replaced by an SSD. On a 4-year-old machine that SSD is almost certainly already inside; by 2022 a
spinning disk in a new laptop was a budget-shelf exception. “Just put an SSD in it” is how you
spend $155 swapping an SSD for another SSD and feel nothing.

So measure before you shop. Sixty seconds: press Start, type
Defragment and Optimize Drives, open it, read the Media type
column. It says one of two things.

  1. Hard disk drive — you are in the minority, and that swap is the best thing
    you can do to this laptop.
  2. Solid state drive — stop shopping. No part saves this machine; your answer
    is in the four steps above.

On a Mac, do not even check. Apple has soldered the drive down since the 2016 MacBook Pro with
Touch Bar, and on Apple Silicon it is encrypted to the M-series chip as well. There is no part to
buy. Read Activity Monitor → Memory →
Memory Pressure instead: green means your Mac uses its memory efficiently, yellow
that it might eventually need more, red that it needs more. Red all day, after these four steps,
means the honest answer is a different Mac.

And the price line you have read a hundred times aged badly. In September 2026 the cheapest
960 GB SATA drive on the trackers — a Kingston A400, the largest that model is made in — was
$155.00. The same drive sold for around $54 in 2024, before NAND contract prices rose 55% to 60% in
a single quarter at the start of 2026. So the swap still pays for itself on a machine with a
spinning disk; it is no longer the $50 afternoon the old articles promise. Memory is worse: a 32 GB
laptop DDR4 kit that ran about $50 in early 2025 costs $150 to $240 now, which is why “add more
RAM” stopped being advice.

How to know it worked: restart once and time it on your phone, power button
to first useful click. A boot that fell from 3 minutes to about 1, with a browser that still
answers you at 12 tabs, is the win. Judge it the next morning — the first boot after a cleanup is
always the slow one.

Do the four steps tonight, in order, before you price a single
laptop. Then run the Media type check — those 60 seconds tell you whether

What Is Actually Draining Your Battery (Check This Screen First)

📅 Aug 08, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

It is 4:15 in the afternoon, you are in the pharmacy parking lot with the engine
still running, and you need to call and ask whether the prescription is ready. Your phone is at 9%
with the battery icon already red. It was at 100% when you pulled it off the charger at 7 a.m. No
movie. No game. Two calls and some scrolling in a waiting room, and the thing is dead before
dinner.

So you start flipping switches. Bluetooth off. Background something off. That one app your nephew
swore was “the bad one,” deleted. Next day the phone dies at 4:40 instead of 4:15 — and your text
alerts stopped arriving, and you cannot remember which switch did it.

Here is what almost nobody does first. Your phone has been keeping score the whole time: a ranked
list, a percentage next to every name. One screen. Read it, then change one thing
instead of five things in the dark.

Open the screen that names the culprit

On your iPhone, open Settings → Battery. Give it a few seconds
to finish drawing the chart.

  1. At the top, on iOS 26 and later, sits Daily Usage, comparing today against
    your last seven days. Blue
    means Similar or lower usage. Orange means Higher usage —
    you have burned more than usual by this hour. Either way, your iPhone names the three apps or
    system activities that contributed most.
  2. Scroll down to App and System Activity: every app and system task, each with
    the percentage of your battery it used.
  3. Want more than today? Tap View All Battery Usage, tap a day, scroll down.
    Your iPhone keeps today plus the previous week.
  4. Tap any name to see that app broken out day by day.

Apple redesigned this screen in iOS 26. If your iPhone still shows two tabs named Last 24
Hours and Last 10 Days, you are on an older iOS and there is no Daily
Usage chart — tap Last 24 Hours and read the same list of apps with percentages.
Either way, Settings → Battery is the door.

On Android, open Settings → Battery →
Battery usage, then tap View by apps. If your Settings list has no
Battery line, tap the search box at the top of Settings and type
battery. On a Samsung Galaxy, if Battery is not on the main Settings list,
open Battery and device care → Battery.

One correction before you read that Android chart, because it changes what the numbers mean: it is
not a 24-hour window. Google’s wording is that it shows your battery level
since the last full charge. Top up in the car at noon and you are staring at half a
day, wondering why nothing looks alarming.

Read the small gray words, not the big percentages

On iPhone, some app names carry a short gray label underneath. That label is the whole diagnosis,
and it is why two apps at the same percentage can mean opposite things.

The label What your phone is telling you Your move No label at all The app spent that battery while it was open on your screen. You were looking at it. Nothing is broken. A mirror, not a culprit. Background Activity It worked while you were not watching — playing audio, tracking location, syncing. Worth cutting if you barely open that app. No Cell Coverage or Low Signal Your phone was hunting for a signal, or you used the app where the bars are weak. Get on Wi-Fi in that room. Notifications The app’s notifications woke your phone up. Trim its notifications. Do not uninstall it.

So YouTube parked at the top with no gray label under it is not a defect — it is a receipt for
your afternoon. What should stop you is a fat number with Background Activity under
an app you swear you have not opened all week, or No Cell Coverage on the app you
use in the back bedroom where the bars die.

The catch: the biggest drain has no line of its own

Your screen is one of the heaviest things your phone runs, and it gets no row of its own. In
App and System Activity on an iPhone there is no line called
Display. The cost of a bright screen is smeared across the percentages of every app
you had open. On Android it hides behind a different button — you see non-app
consumption only after switching from View by apps to
View by systems, and most people never tap it.

That is why “I deleted that app and nothing changed” keeps happening. The list shows Facebook on
top, you delete Facebook, and the phone still quits at 4:15 — because what emptied it was two hours
of a lit screen, whatever was on it.

And do not switch off automatic brightness. It gets blamed constantly, and the advice is
backwards. Apple’s guidance for a phone that dies too fast is to enable
auto-brightness, at Settings → Accessibility →
Display & Text Size → Auto-Brightness. Google says the same
for Android: keep Adaptive brightness on. Same for
Adaptive Battery and the per-app Optimized setting — Google
warns that taking an app off optimization lets it run more and use more. Restricting by hand
shortens your day.

Now change the two things the list pointed at

Cut screen time, not screen quality. On a Pixel: Settings →
Display & touch → Screen timeout → 30 seconds.
On a Samsung Galaxy the same setting lives in Settings → Display →
Screen timeout. Set to 30 seconds instead of two minutes, the screen stops burning
power every time you put the phone down mid-sentence — and you will never notice it happening. On
iPhone, nudge the brightness slider down in Settings →
Display & Brightness.

Stop the background worker you found. On iPhone: Settings →
General → Background App Refresh, tap Background App
Refresh again, then pick Off, Wi-Fi, or Wi-Fi
& Cellular Data. That same screen lets you switch it off for one app instead of all —
do that first. On Android: Settings → Apps →
See all apps → the app → App battery usage →
Allow background usage, left on Optimized. If one app is clearly
misbehaving today, use Force stop → OK instead.

Fix the location hog. On iPhone: Settings →
Privacy & Security → Location Services, tap the app, turn off
Precise Location. On means your exact spot, off means approximate. A coupon app
does not need exact.

Buy back the last two hours. On iPhone 15 or later: Settings →
Battery → Power Mode, which holds Low Power Mode
and Adaptive Power. On iPhone 14 or earlier: Settings →
Battery → Low Power Mode. Two honest details: Low Power Mode shuts
itself off once you charge past 80%, and on a ProMotion display it caps the screen at 60 frames per
second, so scrolling looks less silky. Adaptive Power exists on iPhone 15 Pro and later, ships on by
default with the iPhone 17 family and iPhone Air, and can flip Low Power Mode on at 20%.

On Android: Settings → Battery → Battery Saver
→ Adaptive Battery, expand it, turn on Use Adaptive Battery. Then
back out and turn on Battery percentage, so the number sits in your status bar and
9% stops ambushing you in a parking lot.

Before you blame the battery itself

Two normal situations look exactly like a dying phone. Google states that after a new setup or a
factory reset, optimization can take up to a few weeks to fully take hold, and that right after a
software update the battery normally drains more than usual for a few days. If your bad week started
the day the phone updated itself, wait it out before spending a dollar.

If this has run for months, check the wear. On iPhone 15 or later: Settings →
Battery → Battery Health. On iPhone 14 or earlier:
Battery Health & Charging. For scale, Apple designed iPhone 14 and earlier to
keep 80% of capacity at 500 full charge cycles, and iPhone 15 models to keep 80% at 1,000. On a
Pixel 6 or newer: Settings → Battery →
Battery diagnostics → Battery draining too quickly.

How to know it worked: do not judge by feel, because feel lies. Tomorrow evening, reopen
Settings → Battery. On iPhone, Daily Usage
should read blue instead of orange, and the app you fixed should have slid down the list. On
Android, the name you acted on should no longer sit near the top of View by apps.
Then the real test: what percentage are you at when you sit down to eat? 30% instead of 9% means
you found it.

Battery, write down the top three names, and change one thing. Five minutes, and it
