One of The Big Bang Theory’s most memorable Christmas gifts came with an unexpected connection to Leonard Nimoy himself.

In the Season 2 episode “The Bath Item Gift Hypothesis,” Penny, played by Kaley Cuoco, gives Sheldon, played by Jim Parsons, a napkin signed by Nimoy. Penny explains that the actor had visited her restaurant and used the napkin to wipe his mouth.

That detail sends Sheldon into delighted disbelief. Because the napkin may contain Nimoy’s DNA, Sheldon realizes that he has theoretically moved closer to being able to clone the actor. The exchange delivers both comedy and an unusually touching payoff to Sheldon’s admiration for Nimoy and his Star Trek character, Spock.

But the napkin was more than a standard prop created for the episode. Nimoy had personally supplied it after the show’s writers contacted him.

How Leonard Nimoy contributed to the episode

Discussing the story with Collider, Nimoy confirmed that the production had asked him for the unusual item.

“Some time ago, they asked me if I would provide a napkin that I had used, and I did,” he said. “It has become one of the most highly talked about and most highly enjoyed episodes of all time, I understand.”

The contribution gave the scene another layer: Sheldon’s fictional gift was built around an actual object provided by the person he revered. For viewers, it looked like one more pop-culture gag in a series filled with them. Behind the scenes, however, Nimoy had directly helped make the joke possible.

The moment arrived early in the comedy’s 12-season run, during which The Big Bang Theory repeatedly drew on geek culture. Its many references included Star Wars, Marvel, DC and Lord of the Rings, but Star Trek — and Spock in particular — occupied an especially prominent place in Sheldon’s world.

Nimoy later found another way onto the show

The napkin was not Nimoy’s final contribution to The Big Bang Theory. In Season 5, he voiced a Spock action figure that speaks to Sheldon during a dream sequence, an idea that delighted series creator Chuck Lorre.

Nimoy never appeared physically on the series, although he said an in-person role had been considered. The voice performance instead provided a workable way for him to participate.

“For various reasons, a physical appearance didn’t work out, but when they came up with this idea of voicing the Spock character with Sheldon being given a ‘Star Trek’ transporter, I thought the whole idea was wonderful,” Nimoy told Collider. “It was a way for me to deliver an appearance on the show and to work with that very, very talented bunch of people.”

Other Star Trek cast members eventually appeared on the comedy in person. George Takei, who played Sulu, portrayed himself in Season 4. William Shatner, identified with Captain James T. Kirk, later appeared in Season 12 after the writers agreed to a condition he had set.

ShowBiz Take

The Nimoy napkin stands out because it was not merely another name-check or replica tucked into the background. The writers turned Nimoy’s real contribution into the centerpiece of a scene built around Sheldon’s intense affection for Spock and the actor who played him.

It also shows how The Big Bang Theory could make its pop-culture references part of the storytelling rather than simply use them as decoration. Penny’s gift drives the episode’s emotional and comic payoff, while the prop’s real origin gives the scene an extra off-screen story.

Nimoy’s later voice role completed that connection in a different form. Although a physical appearance never came together, the used napkin and the talking Spock figure allowed him to become part of the series twice — first through an unexpectedly authentic prop, and later through his own voice.

Kevin Bleyer, a four-time Emmy Award-winning writer best known for his work on The Daily Show, has passed away after sustaining injuries in a car crash. He was 54 years old.

The late writer’s publicist told The Hollywood Reporter that he was driving on a rural road in Millrift, Pennsylvania, on Oct. 25 when he lost control and crashed. No one else was hurt.

In addition to winning multiple Emmy Awards, Bleyer received a Peabody Award and a Writers Guild Award. He wrote for The Daily Show with Jon Stewart from 2005 to 2013 and earned all four of his Emmy Awards for the show. He was among several Daily Show writers to write for the 80th Annual Academy Awards in 2008. Stewart hosted the award show.

Before The Daily Show, Bleyer was a writer and producer on Politically Incorrect with Bill Maher from 1996 to 2002. He also wrote the Bravo series Significant Others and created/executive produced the Showtime pilot Nightly. 

After his time on The Daily Show, Bleyer wrote for two seasons on Fox’s Sleepy Hollow.

Bleyer wrote Me The People: One Man’s Selfless Quest to Rewrite the Constitution and was a co-author of Earth: The Book with former New Mexico Governor Bill Richardson. He contributed to President Barack Obama’s speeches.

Honoring Bleyer, actor and screenwriter Danny Strong shared in a statement, “Kevin Bleyer was a writer’s writer. From his many years on The Daily Show to writing Obama’s WHCD speeches to his many books and his work on numerous scripted dramas, Kevin could literally do it all. His immense talent was only outmatched by his kind soul. I am in complete shock that he is gone, and my sadness is overwhelming. I already miss him dearly.”

Bleyer is survived by his wife, Nella; father, Archie; brother, Keith; sister-in-law, Kelly; nephew, Evan; and niece, Lauren.

“Kevin spent his life finding the perfect words to make people laugh, think and see the world a little differently,” Nella said in her statement. “I was lucky enough to know the humor, curiosity, mischief and tenderness behind those words — and to be loved by a man who made my own world more joyful, more adventurous and infinitely more beautiful.”

Why I Keep My Passwords in Two Password Managers

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The short version

  • Every password is generated by a manager and is at least 24 characters, and I add a short ending that I never store in any app.
  • Bitwarden is my main manager and Proton Pass is my backup. I move the data across by hand every few days.
  • My two-step codes live in a separate app, not in the managers.
  • I once used one easy password everywhere. A leak at another company took my accounts down one by one.

For years I used the same email address and the same password everywhere. Short, easy to type, easy to remember. I figured nobody would bother with me.

What mistake started all this?

A big platform got hacked. It made the news around the world, and the stolen data included usernames and passwords. I never read past the headline. You think it won’t happen to you.

Then the services I had signed up for with that email and that password started falling, one by one. My guess is that someone took the stolen pairs and tried them on other sites. I never confirmed it. I can’t even tell you which platform it was, because it was years ago.

That is when I decided to use passwords so strong that I couldn’t know them myself.

Why do I use two password managers?

Bitwarden is my main one. I have used it for many years, and it was the first manager I ever tried. It works well in the apps and in the browser extensions. A few times I have even been a beta tester.

Proton Pass is my backup.

Why two? Things go wrong. I trust Bitwarden a lot, and I still keep a copy. A saying I like: don’t trust, verify.

Every few days I export my vault from Bitwarden and import it into Proton Pass. The transfer is simple. I don’t know whether the two can sync on their own, so I do it by hand. One warning: a standard export is a readable file. I delete it as soon as the import finishes.

Don’t trust, verify.

What rules do I follow?

Every password is at least 24 characters, symbols included. The manager generates it. I never make one up.

Then I do one more thing. The generated password is not the one I log in with. At the end of every password I add five or six characters that are not stored in any app or note. If someone got into Bitwarden or Proton Pass, the passwords they found would still be missing that ending. Some people call it a pepper. I also have a way back if I ever forget it, with people I trust. Without that, forgetting it would lock me out of everything.

It sounds like a chore. It is a few extra keystrokes. I add it at the end because after autofill the cursor is already there. At the start I would have to move it every time.

My two-step verification codes are in 2FAS, an open source app, and not in the password managers. I also use physical security keys, which deserve their own article.

What happens if I lose my phone?

I have this ready. I sign in to Bitwarden from another device, then close every session I can: Bitwarden, Proton, everything else. Even if someone got to the vaults, the passwords would not be enough to log in, because the ending is not in them.

How do I share a password with someone?

With Proton Pass, mostly because I like how it looks. It takes a couple of clicks. You send a link, choose when it expires and limit how many times it can be opened. Set a low number of views and it stops working once they are used.

I have never used a team plan, only personal ones. They have worked very well, but I have not tested a team plan, so I won’t recommend one. For a team, what I would try first is simple: stop sending passwords over WhatsApp or in a spreadsheet, and send a one-use link.

The alias covers your email address. This covers the other half.

This is my experience, not security advice. Features and plans change, so check each provider.

Deleted a Photo by Accident? You Have About 30 Days.

📅 Aug 21, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

You were on the couch thumbing through your camera roll, clearing out eleven near-identical shots of a parking lot, and your thumb went one photo too far. The picture slid off the screen — your father at the kitchen table, the last one anybody ever took of him. Here is what nobody tells you in that moment: on your iPhone, on your Galaxy and in Google Photos, deleting a photo does not erase it. It moves it. It sits in a holding folder for about 30 days, waiting for you. Most people who “lost” a photo forever never opened that folder.

If you learned somewhere that Google Photos holds deleted pictures for 60 days, stop planning around that number. Google’s own support pages now say 30 days — on Android, on iPhone, on the computer, for photos that were backed up and for photos that never were. Open your own trash, read what your app says about time remaining, and plan for 30.

Do these three things first, in this order

Your photo is almost certainly still on your phone right now. What kills it is the next fifteen minutes of well-meant activity: tidying up, freeing space, installing a recovery app that wants your card number and full access to your gallery before it will show you a single thumbnail.

  1. Open the trash inside the app you deleted from — Photos on the iPhone, Google Photos, Samsung Gallery. Each keeps its own.
  2. Open the trash of whatever backs your phone up: Google Photos, iCloud, OneDrive.
  3. Check the computer you plug that phone into. Any photo you once imported into a folder there is a separate copy.
Do not empty any trash today. Not to free space, not because a pop-up suggests it. Emptying the bin is the one action here that cannot be undone — by you, by Apple, by Google, by any app on any store. Google says it plainly: items that are permanently deleted can’t be restored. Leave every bin exactly as it is until your photo is back in your library.

iPhone and iPad: the album is locked, which is why you never found it

Apple keeps your deleted photos in an album called Recently Deleted, and since iOS 16 that album is locked behind Face ID — which is why you scrolled right past it. Two minutes, start to finish.

  1. Open Photos, then tap Collections at the bottom.
  2. Swipe up and find Recently Deleted in the Utilities section. That album is the destination — if a future update shuffles the tabs again, Utilities is the word to hunt for.
  3. Tap View Album, then unlock it with Face ID or Touch ID.
  4. Tap Select, then tap the photo you want to keep.
  5. Tap the More button — the three dots — then tap Recover. To bring everything back at once, tap Recover All.
  6. Tap Recover again to confirm.

Apple’s deadline leaves no room to negotiate: anything in that album stays 30 days, and after 30 days it’s permanently deleted and you can’t get it back. If your iPhone is dead or drowned, the same album lives elsewhere. On a Mac, open Photos and select Recently Deleted in the Utilities section of the sidebar, unlock with Touch ID, click the photo, click Recover. In a browser, sign in at iCloud.com, open Photos, click Recently Deleted in the sidebar and click Recover.

Google Photos: press and hold, don’t tap

This is where most people quit after ten seconds, because tapping a photo in the trash just opens it and nothing useful appears. The gesture is a press and hold.

  1. Open the Google Photos app on your Android phone, your iPhone or your iPad.
  2. At the bottom, tap Collections, then Trash. Some phones label it Bin — same place, same steps.
  3. Touch and hold the photo or video you want back until it’s selected.
  4. At the bottom, tap Restore.

On a computer, go to photos.google.com/login, click Trash on the left, put your cursor over the photo and click Select — the small circle that appears in the photo’s top left corner when you hover — then click Restore at the top right. Your photo goes back to three places at once: the albums it was in, your Google Photos library, and your phone’s gallery app.

Samsung Galaxy: three doors into the same bin

One thing to know before you start, because Samsung’s own instructions disagree with each other depending on how old your phone is: on newer Galaxy phones the recycle bin is simply on, but on some One UI versions it is a switch you had to flip yourself. Don’t guess — open it and look. Ninety seconds.

  1. Open Gallery and tap the Menu — the three horizontal lines.
  2. Tap Recycle bin. Depending on your phone, the label reads Trash or Bin instead.
  3. Tap Edit. On newer Galaxy phones there is no Edit button — just touch and hold the photo until a checkmark appears on it.
  4. Select the items you want to keep, then tap Restore — or Restore All.

If your Gallery doesn’t show that menu, two other doors open the same bin: My Files → Recycle bin → Edit → Restore, or the long road through Settings → Device care → Storage management → Recycle bin. Items stay 30 days, then they’re deleted automatically.

And if there is no Recycle bin anywhere in Gallery, that’s your answer for this photo — it went straight out, so check the Google Photos trash above instead. Then turn the bin on so tonight is the last time this happens: Gallery → Menu → Settings → Recycle bin.

The catch nobody tells you

The clock started when you deleted it, not when you noticed. The countdown is stamped on the file the second it lands in the bin, and a cleanup job does the subtraction. Nothing restarts it when you finally realize what’s missing. If the photo vanished during a cleanup three weeks ago, you don’t have 30 days — you have about 9. Look tonight, not this weekend.

The trash frees up no space at all, and that’s the trap. Deleted photos keep occupying your storage until they expire, so your phone goes right on complaining that it’s full. You search for a fix, you find “empty the trash,” you tap it — and that single tap is the only irreversible move available to you. Worse: if Google Photos shows you a Delete permanently prompt while you’re merely moving pictures to the trash, your trash is already full and those photos are skipping the bin entirely. Zero days of grace.

Deleting on one device deletes it on all of them. The hope everybody has — “it’s probably still on the tablet” — is almost always false. With iCloud Photos on, Apple deletes that photo on every device signed in with the same Apple Account. Google goes further: deleting in the app also removes the copy sitting on your phone, and with backup on, the copy on every other device too.

If it came from a Shared Library, the button may not be yours. Apple only lets the person who added a photo to a Shared Library recover it or delete it for good. If your wife added it and you deleted it, you have to ask her — and those 30 days keep running while you two sort it out. Call her today.

The backup on your computer may hold no photos at all. Plugging your iPhone into the PC and making a backup is not the same as copying your pictures. With iCloud Photos on, your photos live in the sync, and that backup file can be enormous without containing the one you want. What saves you is importing photos as files into a folder — Pictures on Windows, the Photos app or Image Capture on a Mac.

How to know it actually worked

Two checks, thirty seconds. Find the photo in your main library — it returns to its original date, not to the end of the roll, so scroll to the day it was taken, not to today. Then go back into the bin: it should be gone from there. If it’s still sitting in the trash, tap Restore or Recover again. Then open it and use Share to send it somewhere else — a photo that exists in one app only is one thumb-slip from this same afternoon.

Open the trash on your phone right now — Recently Deleted, Trash or Recycle bin — and look at what’s in there. Some of it you meant to delete. Some of it you didn’t, and you’ve spent weeks assuming it was gone. You have about 30 days from the day each one disappeared, and that clock has been running the whole time.

How to Move Your Contacts to a New Phone (Without Losing the Old Numbers)

📅 Aug 02, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

The new phone is out of the box on your kitchen table, still smelling like packaging. You signed in and opened Contacts. Forty-one names. You had more than four hundred. Your dentist is gone. The neighbor who feeds the cat is gone. Nothing was deleted, though. Those numbers are still on the old phone in the drawer — they just never belonged to the account you signed into, so they were never invited along. Ten minutes on that old phone, before you put it away, is the whole difference.

First, find out where your contacts actually live

Your contacts app shows one clean list. Behind it sit three storage places that look identical: your Google Account or iCloud, the phone’s own memory, and the SIM card. Only the account travels. Google says it plainly: contacts saved in device storage don’t sync anywhere, and if that device is lost or damaged, those contacts may be lost with it. Five minutes tells you how big your problem is.

On your Android phone:

  1. Open Contacts. At the bottom, tap Organize (older versions call this tab Fix & manage — Google renamed it, and its own help pages still disagree).
  2. Tap Manage SIM. Everything on that screen lives on the SIM card and only there.

Do this while the old phone still turns on. Contacts that were already in your Google Account have a 30-day safety net (below). Contacts that lived only on that phone or its SIM have none — once it’s wiped or traded in, they’re gone for good.

The catch nobody tells you

Almost every “my contacts disappeared” story is the same: numbers that lived on the SIM card, never on the account. And a SIM is a far worse copy than you think. A standard SIM holds about 250 contacts, one number per person, and chops long names off after roughly the first dozen characters. “Maria Work Front Desk” comes back truncated, with no email, no photo, no second number. A SIM isn’t a backup. It’s a shrunken photocopy of one.

The iPhone side breaks the advice your son-in-law gave you. Apple states that your iPhone doesn’t store contacts on the SIM card. It reads a SIM; it never writes to one. So the classic trick — save everything to the SIM, pop it into the new phone — doesn’t exist on iOS, and there’s no export-to-SIM button to hunt for. After years on an iPhone, your SIM is probably empty or frozen with contacts from your last Android.

A 2026 wrinkle finishes that plan. Apple’s US spec sheets for iPhone 17 and iPhone Air say the phone uses advanced eSIM technology and is not compatible with physical SIM cards. No tray means no SIM to insert, and Import SIM Contacts will have nothing to import — check your model’s spec sheet before planning around one. And if your phone runs the manufacturer’s own contacts app, Samsung’s included, these menu names differ. On a Samsung, the same job is Contacts → the three-line menu → Manage contacts → Import or export contacts → Export, and Samsung steers whole-phone moves to Smart Switch.

If you already wiped the old phone, you still have a window. Deleted Google contacts sit in the trash for 30 days before they’re deleted permanently, and contacts.google.com → Settings → Undo changes reverses changes from the last 30 days. Past that, nothing comes back.

Understanding Attribution Models in Telegram Marketing

Attribution models for Telegram marketing have emerged as a critical component for understanding and enhancing campaign effectiveness. The Shift to Multi-Touch and Data-Driven Models

Historically, marketers relied on single-touch attribution models, such as first-click or last-click attribution. However, as user journeys have become more complex, particularly within multi-channel environments like Telegram, there’s been a significant shift towards multi-touch attribution (MTA) and data-driven models. A multi-touch model allows for a more nuanced understanding of how various interactions contribute to conversions.

For instance, a user may first discover a product through an influencer’s Telegram channel, receive a targeted message from a brand’s bot, and finally convert after a follow-up reminder. Tracking these touchpoints helps marketers understand which channels and messages are most effective.

Privacy-Preserving Tracking Necessities

With increasing data privacy regulations, using invasive tracking techniques has become untenable for many marketers. This is particularly relevant on platforms like Telegram, where built-in analytics may not offer a complete picture of user interactions. As a result, marketers are adapting by employing probabilistic and server-side attribution models that respect user privacy while still delivering actionable insights.

Performance Measurement Challenges

Tracking conversions in Telegram’s encrypted and closed ecosystem presents unique challenges. With limited built-in analytics, marketers often need to create custom tracking solutions that accurately account for user engagement, such as clicks on messages or joining specific groups. This process often involves the integration of third-party analytics tools to gather performance data and assess the effectiveness of various campaigns.

Competitor Strategies for Attribution in Telegram Marketing

Successful Telegram marketers and affiliate campaigns have developed strategies to navigate these challenges. Here are some common practices:

Custom UTM Codes and Referral Links

Marketers utilize unique UTM codes embedded in links shared via Telegram channels and bots. This practice allows brands to identify which traffic sources directly lead to conversions. By tagging links with channel-specific identifiers, businesses can better track the performance of their Telegram content.

First-Touch and Last-Touch Models

While some brands favor last-touch attribution—crediting the final interaction before conversion—others find value in first-touch models that recognize the initial exposure. This dual approach can be particularly beneficial when evaluating the journey of users who may take time before making a decision to convert.

Off-Platform Event Monitoring

Successful case studies illustrate that exporting engagement data—like message opens, clicks, and replies—from Telegram and correlating it with web analytics can provide a clearer picture of user behavior. This combined data analysis enables marketers to gauge the effectiveness of their outreach efforts beyond the platform itself.

Two Russian missiles struck the Ukrainian capital Kyiv after U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres ended talks with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky focusing on how to save servicemen and civilians still trapped in the besieged and ruined port of Mariupol.

One missile struck an apartment building, injuring at least 10 people.

Guterres acknowledged the failure of U.N. institutions, primarily the Security Council, to resolve the humanitarian crisis in the port – earlier this week he said he had secured a commitment from Kremlin leader Vladimir Putin to allow the U.N. to oversee an evacuation.

Russian forces made some progress in their reoriented campaign to capture all of eastern Ukraine’s Donbas region, particularly around the northeastern city of Izyum.

In Washington, U.S. President Joe Biden announced a $33 billion package of military, financial and humanitarian aid for Ukraine – a substantial increase. Zelensky expressed thanks and said he hoped Congress would approve the package quickly.

In his nightly address to boost the morale of his people, Zelensky was indignant at the Russian missile attack just after Guterres had wrapped up his talks on humanitarian solutions.

“This says a lot about Russia’s true attitude toward global institutions, about the efforts of the Russian leadership to humiliate the U.N. and everything that the organisation represents,” Zelensky said. “It requires a strong response.”

U.N. help to evacuate Mariupol

Zelensky said he believed that with U.N. help a solution could be found to evacuate soldiers still holed up in the vast Azovstal steel works in Mariupol, along with large numbers of civilians. City officials say tens of thousands of city residents are also awaiting evacuation from a town all but flattened by weeks of Russian encirclement and bombardment.

Ukraine, the president said, “is ready for these steps, but it is also necessary for the Russian side to consider this issue without cynicism and actually do what it says. Moscow claimed they had allegedly ceased fire in Mariupol, but the bombing of the defenders of the city continues.”

Guterres was contrite in acknowledging the failure of the Security Council – where Russia has used its power as a permanent member to throw out a series of resolutions — to stop the fighting.

“Let me be very clear. The Security Council failed to do everything in its power to prevent and end this war,” he told reporters. “This is a source of great disappointment, frustration and anger.”

But Guterres said he would not give up and pledged to expand the U.N.’s assistance programmes by distributing $100 million per month, reaching 1.3 million people by May and covering 2 million by August.

He described Mariupol as “a crisis within a crisis. Thousands of civilians need life-saving assistance…They need an escape route out of the apocalypse.”

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said civilians were free to leave Mariupol, a key target that would enable the Russian military to build a corridor for its troops from Crimea, annexed by Moscow in 2014 to two enclaves seized by Kremlin proxies in the same year. But Ukrainian servicemen, he said, would have to give themselves up.

Russian forces became bogged down in their initial attempt to advance on Kyiv and regrouped and withdrew to the north to launch a new assault on Donbas. Their forces have suffered considerable and unexpected losses – Zelensky has said those losses now exceed the 15,000 Soviet soldiers who died in the 10-year campaign in Afghanistan.

A research group, the Institute for the Study of War, said Russian forces were registering  “minor but steady” advances, but were having difficulty breaking through Ukrainian defences. U.S. assistance and lend-lease legislation

Wake Island

Wake Island, formerly Halcyon Island, coral atoll, territory of the United States, central Pacific Ocean. It is a group of three islets (Wake, Peale, and Wilkes) that enclose a shallow lagoon. First sighted by Spanish explorers in 1568, the island was visited by the British in 1796. Wake Island was formally occupied by the United States in 1899. In 1934 it was placed under the jurisdiction of the U.S. Department of the Navy, and in 1935 a commercial air base was established on the atoll to serve planes on flights between the United States and Asia. Construction of a naval air base and a submarine base on Wake Island was begun in 1939.

On December 7, 1941, during World War II, immediately after the Japanese surprise attack on Pearl Harbor, Japanese air and naval forces attacked Wake Island. After a heroic defense against overwhelming odds, the small garrison surrendered on December 23. The U.S. flag was again raised over Wake Island in September 1945, following the Japanese surrender. The island is administered by the U.S. Department of the Interior, although most activities are overseen by the U.S. Air Force. In 2006 a major typhoon struck the island, forcing a complete evacuation and heavily damaging the air base. The facilities were subsequently rebuilt. Alexandr Misko – New-Age Experimental Percussive Fingerstyle Guitar Redefining how an Acoustic Guitar Can be played Alexandr Misko – New-Age Experimental Percussive Fingerstyle Guitarist

Meet Alexandr Misko

Alexandr Misko is a self-described "New-age experimental percussive fingerstyle Guitar player" from Russia who has developed a remarkably distinctive approach to the acoustic guitar.

His musical journey began at the age of 12 when his parents gave him his first classical guitar on New Year's Eve. Before that, Alexandr admits that much of his time was spent playing video games on his PlayStation. But the arrival of that guitar changed everything.

Alexandr began taking classical guitar lessons and spent three years studying the instrument. However, after discovering the world of fingerstyle guitar, he eventually left his formal lessons behind and began developing his own sound through independent exploration and experimentation.

By the age of 20, Alexandr had already become one of the most exciting young percussive fingerstyle guitarists in the world. His playing combines fingerstyle, tapping, percussion, rhythm, unusual time signatures, polyrhythms and innovative techniques that push the acoustic guitar far beyond traditional expectations.

With millions of views on YouTube and a growing international audience, Alexandr's music demonstrates that technical ability and emotional songwriting do not have to exist separately. His goal is not simply to show what he can do with a guitar. Instead, he wants to create music that makes people feel something.

An Interview With Alexandr Misko

Alexandr's music is difficult to place into a single category. His influences stretch from American hip-hop and old-school rap to deathcore, progressive metal and doom metal. At the same time, he draws inspiration from minimalist and avant-garde composers including Steve Reich, Erik Satie, Philip Glass, Graham Fitkin and John Cage.

His guitar influences include some of the most respected names in modern fingerstyle guitar, including Don Ross, Mike Dawes, Jon Gomm, Pino Forastiere, Thomas Leeb and Preston Reed.

However, Alexandr is careful not to simply imitate the artists who inspire him. He believes that drawing inspiration from many different genres allows him to create something that is uniquely his own.

Rather than defining himself solely by his technical abilities, Alexandr describes the genre of his music as "Progressive Pop." His goal is to create music that remains melodic and accessible while incorporating progressive elements such as complicated structures, unusual time signatures and polyrhythms.

STAGE PASS was recently able to pry Alexandr away from writing his university exams to tell us more about himself.

The Interview

So tell us, what age did you first start playing guitar?

Alexandr: I started playing guitar at the age of 12. My parents presented me my first classical guitar on the New Year’s Eve. Before that I was doing nothing and just playing videogames all day long. So now I’m 20 and I’m so glad that I gave up playing playstation back then ha-ha!

What first interested you in playing the guitar?

Alexandr: I have never asked anybody to buy me a guitar or any other musical instrument, but it was obvious that I have always shown interest in music since early childhood. I was really into American hip-hop and rap music of East & West coast! Moreover, I tried to find biographies and lyrics, and sometimes I used to record some of my raps on my phone along with my friend. We were mimicking American slang that we had been hearing on those songs and tried to come up with something similar to be cool. That was fun! I guess that at the age of 10-12 was the first spark.

Were you self taught or did you take lessons?

Alexandr: When I got my first guitar I decided to take private lessons with a tutor straight away. We were studying classical guitar for 3 years and to be honest I have never been an excellent student. But when I finally got acquainted with “fingerstyle”, I completely abandoned my lessons and started to learn it on my own.

Your guitar playing covers a variety of styles including fingerstyle and tapping yet also incorporates rhythm. I don't think I've seen people use the tuning peg to make note adjustments while playing a song before. What were your influences and how did you end up with the style that seems to be uniquely yours?

Alexandr: Basically everything that I can do now is a blend of other guitarists’ styles combined. I know one thing for sure - I couldn't do anything decent without learning songs and gaining experience from such great composers as Don Ross, Mike Dawes, Jon Gomm, Pino Forastiere, Thomas Leeb, Preston Reed, Steve Reich and many others. They are all my heroes and have been inspiring me since then.

But of course I get inspiration not only from other guitarists because it can make you sound exactly like your idol, and for sure you don’t want to be someone’s musical clone. That’s why I’m always trying to find something new and interesting in other genres that can be really far away from guitar playing and to use it to make my own tunes unique.

For example, I’m fond of heavy genres like Deathcore, Prog metal, Doom Metal. Also I love minimalistic avant-garde music of 19th and 20th centuries, for example such composers as Steve Reich, Erik Satie, Philip Glass, Graham Fitkin, John Cage. I still love American old school rap and write some lyrics in Russian…

Eventually I will be able to name at least one favorite artist in every existing genre of music. At least that's my goal

What do you personally call your style of guitar playing?

Alexandr: There are so many things in my style of playing so an appropriate name would be long and boring like “New-age experimental percussive fingerstyle”, but it roughly describes only technical side of my music.

I try to write music, not a technique, so in terms of genre I would call it “Progressive Pop” because I’m trying to write melodic and accessible music with some progressive flavor that means the use of complicated structures, unusual time signatures, polyrhythms and other stuff.
